Translation

“Once instruction in Brahman has taken place, one should perform duties beginning with Sandhyā.” Gopīnātha introduces this as a statement of Jaimini. He says that the obligation to undertake Sandhyopāsanā following that instruction has already been shown. In response to the question of how that worship is to be carried out, he now explains it, first presenting the nature of the three Sandhyās.

Textual location (p. 216)

Gopīnātha introduces an extended quotation with तत्र संध्यास्वरूपं प्राह श्रुतिःtatra saṃdhyāsvarūpaṃ prāha śrutiḥtatra saMdhyAsvarUpaM prAha shrutiH𑌤𑌤𑍍𑌰 𑌸𑌂𑌧𑍍𑌯𑌾𑌸𑍍𑌵𑌰𑍂𑌪𑌂 𑌪𑍍𑌰𑌾𑌹 𑌶𑍍𑌰𑍁𑌤𑌿𑌃తత్ర సంధ్యాస్వరూపం ప్రాహ శ్రుతిఃತತ್ರ ಸಂಧ್ಯಾಸ್ವರೂಪಂ ಪ್ರಾಹ ಶ್ರುತಿಃതത്ര സന്ധ്യാസ്വരൂപം പ്രാഹ ശ്രുതിഃ, beginning:

रक्षा सि ह वा पुरोनुवाके तपोग्रमतिष्ठन्तrakṣā si ha vā puronuvāke tapogramatiṣṭhantarakShA si ha vA puronuvAke tapogramatiShThanta𑌰𑌕𑍍𑌷𑌾 𑌸𑌿 𑌹 𑌵𑌾 𑌪𑍁𑌰𑍋𑌨𑍁𑌵𑌾𑌕𑍇 𑌤𑌪𑍋𑌗𑍍𑌰𑌮𑌤𑌿𑌷𑍍𑌠𑌨𑍍𑌤రక్షా సి హ వా పురోనువాకే తపోగ్రమతిష్ఠంతರಕ್ಷಾ ಸಿ ಹ ವಾ ಪುರೋನುವಾಕೇ ತಪೋಗ್ರಮತಿಷ್ಠಂತരക്ഷാ സി ഹ വാ പുരോനുവാകേ തപോഗ്രമതിഷ്ഠന്ത ...

The passage describes beings opposing the sun, waters consecrated with the Gāyatrī being thrown upward at Sandhyā, and contemplation of the sun as Brahman. The entire quotation needs a dedicated source-and-variant study; it is not silently reproduced here as a verified Vedic reading.

Gopīnātha subsequently glosses its contemplative import, stating:

अत्राभिध्यानमुपासनम् ।atrābhidhyānamupāsanam .atrAbhidhyAnamupAsanam .𑌅𑌤𑍍𑌰𑌾𑌭𑌿𑌧𑍍𑌯𑌾𑌨𑌮𑍁𑌪𑌾𑌸𑌨𑌮𑍍 ।అత్రాభిధ్యానముపాసనం .ಅತ್ರಾಭಿಧ್ಯಾನಮುಪಾಸನಂ .അത്രാഭിധ്യാനമുപാസനം .

Translation: “Here, sustained meditation is upāsanā.”

He also cites a statement attributed to Vyāsa concerning the non-separation of Gāyatrī and Brahman. This is evidence for the interpretive synthesis in Gopīnātha, not by itself a proof that every detail of a later procedure is directly enjoined in an early Vedic text.

Translation

Parāśara explains the nature of Sandhyā: “The junction of day and night, without the sun or stars [being visible], is called Sandhyā by sages who see the truth.”

Gopīnātha explains that, although the word sandhyā here appears to designate a period of time, it can indicate the deity worshipped at that time. Through the worship of that deity, the word can in turn designate the ritual action. Alternatively, sandhyā simply means an act performed at the junction.

“And Sandhyā is threefold.” Gopīnātha cites Atri: “A twice-born person who knows the Self should always perform the three Sandhyās.”

He then cites Vyāsa for the names distinguished by time: “In the forenoon she is called Gāyatrī, at midday Sāvitrī, and in the evening Sarasvatī; the same Sandhyā is remembered in three forms.”
