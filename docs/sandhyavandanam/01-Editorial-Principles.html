  1. Gopīnātha Dīkṣita's Saṃskāraratnamālā is the central text to be read in order, translated, and cross-referenced. Do not attribute an injunction or a quotation to him until the relevant passage has been checked.
  2. Treat Śruti, Gṛhya/Dharma Sūtra, Smṛti, Nibandha/commentary, and living prayoga as distinct kinds of evidence. These are source categories, not a simple ranking of historical authenticity.
  3. Keep the older attested core visibly distinct from later Purāṇic, Āgamic, regional, and customary additions. Describe those additions fairly and precisely.
  4. Do not turn a mantra found in a Vedic text into evidence that the whole current rite was enjoined there.
  5. For every quotation record: work, recension where relevant, chapter/sūtra/mantra, edition, volume/page or stable URL, text as printed, transcription confidence, translation, and source context.
  6. For every procedural comparison record: selection (Veda, śākhā, sūtra, time of day), attested action/order/mantra, source and confidence, interpretation, and differences.
  7. Mark assertions verified, secondary witness, lead to verify, or editorial synthesis.
  8. Avoid Hindi. Sanskrit headings and English explanatory prose are permitted. Preserve Sanskrit diacritics and Vedic svara when verified.

Reusable status terms

  • Verified: checked against cited primary edition.
  • Witness reported: cited in an identifiable secondary source; original not yet checked.
  • To verify: bibliographic or textual research lead.
  • Not documented: no conclusion has been reached on the practice.
