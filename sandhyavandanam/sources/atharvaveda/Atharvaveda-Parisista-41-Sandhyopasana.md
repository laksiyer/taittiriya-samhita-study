**English translation:** Having thoroughly washed the hands, feet and face, and having sat facing east in vīrāsana, one should sip water with the mantra beginning “Jīvā stha,” sprinkle oneself with “Āpo hi ṣṭhā,” perform prāṇāyāmas, sip water again, and rise. अष्टकृत्व एकादशकृत्वो द्वादशकृत्वः पञ्चदशकृत्वः शतकृत्वः सहस्रकृत्व इति ॥

## ४१.3

### ४१.3.1

अतो यथाकामं जपित्वा पश्येम शरदः शतम् इन्द्र जीवेत्य् आशिषः प्रार्थयते ॥

**English translation:** After thus repeating [the mantra] as desired, one asks for blessings with mantras beginning “Paśyema śaradaḥ śatam” and “Indra jīva.”

</details>

### ४१.3.2

स्तुता मया वरदेति विसृज्योदीराणा उत सूर्यस्यावृतम् असपत्नं पुरस्ताद् यस्मात् कोशाद् इति यथार्थम् उपतिष्ठते ॥

**English translation:** Having dismissed [the invoked deity] with “Stutā mayā varade,” one makes the appropriate upasthāna with mantras beginning “Udīrāṇā,” “Uta sūryasya,” “Āvṛtam asapatnaṃ purastāt,” and “Yasmāt kośāt.” [The exact division into mantra incipits awaits verification.]

<details><summary>IAST (source transcription) · 41.3.2</summary>

stutā mayā varadeti visṛjyodīrāṇā uta sūryasyāvṛtam asapatnaṃ purastād yasmāt kośād iti yathārtham upatiṣṭhate ||

</details>

### ४१.3.3

य इमां न विन्दन्ति नाधीयते संध्याकाले नोपासते ते ह्य् अश्रोत्रिया भवन्त्य् अनुपनीताः क्रियाहीनाश् छेदनभेदनभोजनमैथुनान्य् अभिचरन्तः ॥

**English translation:** Those who do not learn this, do not study, and do not perform worship at Sandhyā time become, according to the text, non-śrotriyas, as though uninitiated and devoid of rites, engaged in cutting, piercing, eating, sexual intercourse, and other conduct. [Syntax and the final compound are uncertain.]

<details><summary>IAST (source transcription) · 41.3.3</summary>

ya imāṃ na vindanti nādhīyate saṃdhyākāle nopāsate te hy aśrotriyā bhavanty anupanītāḥ kriyāhīnāś chedanabhedanabhojanamaithunāny abhicarantaḥ ||

</details>

### ४१.3.4

संध्याकाले ह्य् अजपन्तः श्वसूकरसृगालकुक्कुटसर्पयोनिषु वर्षसहस्राणि जायन्ते ॥

**English translation:** Those who fail to recite at Sandhyā time are said to be born for thousands of years in the wombs of dogs, pigs, jackals, fowl, and snakes.

<details><summary>IAST (source transcription) · 41.3.4</summary>

</details>

### ४१.3.8

सायं संध्याम् उपासीत कृतवीरासनो द्विजः । कृतोत्तानस् तथा प्रातः प्राञ्जलिः सुसमाहितः ॥

**English translation:** A twice-born man should worship the evening Sandhyā having assumed vīrāsana; in the morning he should be upright [or with the prescribed raised posture], hands joined, and thoroughly composed.

### ४१.3.9

एतद् वीरासनं स्थानं ब्रह्मणा निर्मितं पुरा । द्विजानं बालवृद्धानां पुरश्चरणम् उत्तमम् ॥

**English translation:** This posture called vīrāsana was fashioned by Brahmā in former times; it is the excellent preliminary observance for twice-born people, young and old.

### ४१.3.10

**English translation:** Whoever attends upon the evening and morning Sandhyā without interruption is purified by that goddess; even one guilty of Brahmin-slaying is said to be cleansed of sin.

### ४१.3.11

न सीदेत् प्रतिगृह्णानः पृथ्वीम् अपि ससागराम् । ये चास्य विषमाः के चिद् दिवि सूर्यादयो ग्रहाः ॥

**English translation:** One is said not to come to ruin even while accepting the earth together with its oceans; and whatever celestial planets, beginning with the Sun, are adverse to that person ...

### ४१.3.12

ते चास्य सौम्या जायन्ते शिवाः सुखकराः सदा । स्थानं वीरासनं चएषां पृथिवी च प्रदक्षिणा । अग्निहोत्रं हुतं चैषां ये वै संध्याम् उपासते ॥

**English translation:** ... they become gentle, auspicious, and always conducive to happiness. For those who worship Sandhyā, standing and vīrāsana [count as prescribed observances], circumambulation of the earth [is theirs], and their Agnihotra is [as though] performed. [The precise syntax of the last two claims needs checking.]

<details><summary>IAST (source transcription) · 41.3.12</summary>

## ४१.4

### ४१.4.1

अर्धास्तमित आदित्ये अर्धोदिते दिवाकरे । गायत्र्यास् तत्र सांनिध्यं संध्याकालः स उच्यते ॥

**English translation:** When the sun is half set, and when the day-maker is half risen, the Gāyatrī is present there. That is called Sandhyā time.

<details><summary>IAST (source transcription) · 41.4.1</summary>

ardhāstamita āditye ardhodite divākare | gāyatryās tatra sāṃnidhyaṃ saṃdhyākālaḥ sa ucyate ||

</details>

### ४१.4.2

भूम्यादित्यान्तरं यस् तु छादयेच् चतुरङ्गुलम् । तां तु संध्यां परां विद्याच् छायासंभेदने परे ॥

**English translation:** That [time of] Sandhyā should be known as the highest when [the sun’s] separation from the earth is covered by four finger-breadths; others [define it] at the merging of shadows. [This difficult description requires a checked text and commentary.]

<details><summary>IAST (source transcription) · 41.4.2</summary>

bhūmyādityāntaraṃ yas tu chādayec caturaṅgulam | tāṃ tu saṃdhyāṃ parāṃ vidyāc chāyāsaṃbhedane pare ||

</details>

### ४१.4.3

यावन्तस् तु कराद् भ्रष्टाः पतन्ति जलबिन्दवः । भूत्वा वज्राणि ते सर्वे पतन्ति ह्य् असुरेषु वै ॥

**English translation:** As many drops of water as fall from the hand become thunderbolts and fall upon the Asuras.

<details><summary>IAST (source transcription) · 41.4.3</summary>

yāvantas tu karād bhraṣṭāḥ patanti jalabindavaḥ | bhūtvā vajrāṇi te sarve patanti hy asureṣu vai ||

</details>

### ४१.4.4

ततो विभावसुस् तेषां प्रीतात्माप्यायते वरम् । यैर् अहं मोक्षितो विप्रैस् तेषां लोको तथा मम ॥

**English translation:** Then Vibhāvasu (the radiant one, probably the Sun in context), pleased by them, is nourished and grants a boon: “Those Brahmins who released me shall obtain a world like mine.” [Speaker and syntax require confirmation.]

<details><summary>IAST (source transcription) · 41.4.4</summary>

गायत्र्या अक्षमालायां सायं प्रातः शतं जपेत् । चतुर्णां खलु वेदानां समग्रं लभते फलम् ॥

### ४१.4.6

### ४१.4.8

ऋषयो दीर्घसंध्यत्वाद् दीर्घाण्य् आयुम्ष्य् अधारयन् । तस्माद् दीर्घाम् उपासीत समिच्छन् वृद्धिम् आयुषः ॥

## ४१.5

### ४१.5.1

तद् यथाग्निर् देवानां ब्राह्मणो मनुष्याणां वसन्त ऋतूनाम् एवं गायत्री छन्दसाम् ॥

**English translation:** As Agni is foremost among the gods, the Brahmin among human beings, and spring among the seasons, so is Gāyatrī among the metres.

<details><summary>IAST (source transcription) · 41.5.1</summary>

</details>

### ४१.5.2

तद् यथा गायत्री कत्यक्षरा कतिपदा किं वास्या गोत्रं किं वास्य रूपं कीदृशं तस्याः शरीरं भवति ॥

</details>

तद् यथेदम् अक्षरम् ओम् इत्य् अक्षरं तत् परमं शम् इत्य् अक्षरं गुह्यं तत् परमं पवित्रम् ॥

**English translation:** As to this syllable “Om”: that is the supreme syllable. The syllable “śam” is secret; that is supremely purifying. [Segmentation of the sentence is provisional.]

### ४१.5.5

आदित्यो वै सावित्र्य् आदित्येन सह सावित्री स्तौति सुवति प्रातः प्रसुवति तस्मात् सावित्रीत्वम् ॥

**English translation:** The Sun is associated with Sāvitrī. Along with the Sun, Sāvitrī praises, impels, and sets forth in the morning; hence her name Sāvitrī. [The exact play on the verbal root √sū requires philological review.]

</details>

### ४१.5.6

अक्षरदैवतं व्याख्यास्यामः

**English translation:** We shall explain the deity associated with each syllable.

### ४१.5.7

प्रथमम् आग्नेयं द्वितीयम् आश्विनं तृतीयं सौम्यं चतुर्थं वैष्णवं सावित्रं पञ्चमं षष्ठं पौष्णं सप्तमं मारुतम् अष्टमं बार्हस्पत्यं नवमं मैत्रं दशमं वारुणम् एकादशम् ऐन्द्रं द्वादशं वैश्वदेवं वसूनां त्रयोदशं चतुर्दशं रुद्राणां पञ्चदशम् आदित्यानाम् अदितेः षोडशं वायव्यं सप्तदशमं भौमम् अष्टादशम् एकोनविंशम् आन्तरिक्षं दिव्यं विंशं दिग्देवतानि चत्वार्य् अक्षराणि ॥

## ४१.6

### ४१.6.1

अथ यत् पूर्वां संध्याम् उपास्ते तद् गायत्र्याः शिरस् तेन पृथिवीं जयति ॥

### ४१.6.4

तस्या ओंकारः शिरः सह व्याहृतिभिर् दर्भाः केशा ओषधीवनस्पतयो लोमानि चक्षुषी सूर्याचन्द्रमसौ विद्युद् धसितं विष्णुवरुणौ उरसी रुद्रो हृदये पौर्णमासी चामावास्या च स्तनौ अहश् च रात्री च पार्श्वे

**English translation:** Of her, Om together with the vyāhṛtis is the head; darbha grasses are the hair; herbs and trees the body hairs; Sun and Moon her eyes; lightning her smile; Viṣṇu and Varuṇa her breasts or chest [reading uncertain]; Rudra is in her heart; full moon and new moon are her breasts; day and night are her sides.

</details>

### ४१.6.5

दश दिशः कुक्षी सर्वज्ञानानि व्याकरणम् उदरं पृथिवी श्रोणी वायुः स्थानं भूषणं नक्षत्राणि श्रीसरस्वतीरूपा पदक्रममन्त्रब्राह्मणकल्पशरीरा सावित्री गोत्रेण ब्रह्मदेया भवति ब्रह्मदेया भवतीति ब्राह्मणम् ॥

**English translation:** The ten directions are her flanks; all branches of knowledge and grammar [are associated with] her belly; Earth is her hips; Wind her station; the stars her ornaments. She has the form of Śrī and Sarasvatī; her body consists of the pada and krama recitations, mantras, Brāhmaṇas, and Kalpa. Sāvitrī is said to be a gift of Brahman by lineage, a gift of Brahman indeed. Thus ends the Brāhmaṇa. [Several nominal identifications in this compressed passage remain uncertain.]

daśa diśaḥ kukṣī sarvajñānāni vyākaraṇam udaraṃ pṛthivī śroṇī vāyuḥ sthānaṃ bhūṣaṇaṃ nakṣatrāṇi śrīsarasvatīrūpā padakramamantrabrāhmaṇakalpaśarīrā sāvitrī gotreṇa brahmadeyā bhavati brahmadeyā bhavatīti brāhmaṇam ||

</details>
