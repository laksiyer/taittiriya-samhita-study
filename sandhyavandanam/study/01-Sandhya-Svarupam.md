## १. प्रसङ्गः — Why Sandhyā follows Upanayana

### मूलपाठः (p. 216)

> "जाते ब्रह्मोपदेशे तु कर्म संध्यादिकं चरेत्"
>
> इति जैमिनिवचनेनोपदेशोत्तरं संध्योपासनकर्तव्यता पूर्वत्र प्रदर्शिता । तच्चोपासनं कथं कार्यमित्याकाङ्क्षायां संध्यात्रयस्वरूपप्रदर्शनपूर्वकं तदभिधीयते ।

### Translation

“Once instruction in Brahman has taken place, one should perform duties beginning with Sandhyā.” Gopīnātha introduces this as a statement of Jaimini. He says that the obligation to undertake Sandhyopāsanā following that instruction has already been shown. In response to the question of *how* that worship is to be carried out, he now explains it, first presenting the nature of the three Sandhyās.

### अध्ययनटीका

- This transition is explicitly situated within the *Upanayana* discussion. It is not an isolated prayer-book introduction.
- The passage is **attributed to Jaimini by Gopīnātha**. The exact external location of this quotation remains to be verified.
- The threefold discussion is introduced here **before** the detailed ritual sequence. We should preserve that order.

## २. श्रुतिवचनम् तथा उपासनार्थः — Vedic testimony and interpretation

### Textual location (p. 216)

Gopīnātha introduces an extended quotation with *तत्र संध्यास्वरूपं प्राह श्रुतिः*, beginning:

> रक्षा सि ह वा पुरोनुवाके तपोग्रमतिष्ठन्त ...

The passage describes beings opposing the sun, waters consecrated with the Gāyatrī being thrown upward at Sandhyā, and contemplation of the sun as Brahman. **The entire quotation needs a dedicated source-and-variant study; it is not silently reproduced here as a verified Vedic reading.**

Gopīnātha subsequently glosses its contemplative import, stating:

> अत्राभिध्यानमुपासनम् ।

**Translation:** “Here, sustained meditation is *upāsanā*.”

He also cites a statement attributed to Vyāsa concerning the non-separation of Gāyatrī and Brahman. This is evidence for the *interpretive synthesis in Gopīnātha*, not by itself a proof that every detail of a later procedure is directly enjoined in an early Vedic text.

## ३. सन्ध्याशब्दस्य अर्थः — What does Sandhyā mean?

### मूलपाठः (p. 217)

> संध्यास्वरूपमाह पराशरः--
>
> "अहोरात्रस्य यः संधिः सूर्यनक्षत्रवर्जितः ।
> सा तु संध्या समाख्याता मुनिभिस्तत्त्वदर्शिभिः" इति ॥
>
> अत्र यद्यपि कालवाचकत्वेन संध्याशब्दः प्रतीयते तथाऽपि तस्मिन्काल उपास्या देवता संध्याशब्देनोपलक्ष्यते । तथा च देवताया उपासनमुपलक्ष्य पराशरवचने कर्मपरत्वेन संध्याशब्दः प्रयुक्तः । अथवा संधौ भवा क्रिया संध्या ।

### Translation

Parāśara explains the nature of Sandhyā: “The junction of day and night, without the sun or stars [being visible], is called Sandhyā by sages who see the truth.”

Gopīnātha explains that, although the word *sandhyā* here appears to designate a period of time, it can indicate the deity worshipped at that time. Through the worship of that deity, the word can in turn designate the ritual action. Alternatively, *sandhyā* simply means an act performed at the junction.

### अध्ययनटीका — A significant interpretive distinction

1. **कालः:** the temporal junction.
2. **उपास्या देवता:** the deity approached in that period.
3. **क्रिया / उपासना:** the act of worship associated with it.

This is Gopīnātha's own stated analysis of the semantic transition. It should anchor our definition of *Sandhyāvandanam* in the manual.

## ४. सन्ध्यात्रैविध्यम् — The three Sandhyās

### मूलपाठः (p. 217)

> सा च संध्या त्रिविधा ।
>
> तदुक्तमत्रिणा--
> "संध्यात्रयं तु कर्तव्यं द्विजेनाऽऽत्मविदा सदा" इति ।
>
> तत्र कालभेदेन देवताया नामादिभेदमाह व्यासः--
> "गायत्री नाम पूर्वाह्णे सावित्री दिनमध्यके ।
> सरस्वती च सायाह्ने सैव संध्या त्रिषु स्मृता ॥"

### Translation

“And Sandhyā is threefold.” Gopīnātha cites Atri: “A twice-born person who knows the Self should always perform the three Sandhyās.”

He then cites Vyāsa for the names distinguished by time: “In the forenoon she is called Gāyatrī, at midday Sāvitrī, and in the evening Sarasvatī; the same Sandhyā is remembered in three forms.”

### अध्ययनटीका

- This is an **explicit threefold account in Gopīnātha**, employing verses he attributes to Atri and Vyāsa.
- Their precise external loci and variant readings remain to be established.
- A modern observer should not infer from this quotation alone that every early Gṛhyasūtra contains three parallel full procedures. That remains an independent investigation.
- The remainder of printed p. 217 continues with explanations for the names and differences of color; these will be handled in the next installment together with the continuation onto p. 218.

## ६. Questions for continued study

1. Identify the full original source and recension of the quotation introduced as *śruti* on p. 216; check the sun, water, Gāyatrī, and *asāv ādityo brahma* portions independently.
2. Where do the Parāśara, Vyāsa, Atri, and Jaimini verses occur in surviving editions? Does their wording vary?
3. How is midday Sandhyā supported in Baudhāyana, Āpastamba, and Hiraṇyakeśin sources?
4. Does the *Sandhyābhāṣyasamuccaya* discuss the temporal, deity, and action senses of *sandhyā*?
5. How do the passages of Manu on the morning and evening Sāvitrī intersect with this threefold system?
