# तैत्तिरीयब्राह्मणम् १.१.१

## Editorial introduction

The first anuvāka begins with mantras for the Śukra and Manthin Soma vessels. Sāyaṇa explains why these supplementary mantras precede the Agnyādhāna discussion. The opening invocatory verses belong to **Sāyaṇa's commentary**, not to the accented Brāhmaṇa mūla.

## Unit 1 — Brahman, Kṣatra, and the blessings of prosperity

### Mūla (accented, excerpt verified against independent digital source; TB 1.1.1.1)

ब्रह्म॒ संध॑त्तं॒ तन्मे॑ जिन्वतम् । क्ष॒त्रꣳ संध॑त्तं॒ तन्मे॑ जिन्वतम् ।

*Source: [Sanskrit Documents, Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa (with accents)](https://sanskritdocuments.org/doc_veda/taittirIyabrAhmaNam.html), TB 1.1.1.1. The remainder of the sequence should be checked directly at the source before insertion.*

### English translation of the mūla

“Join together brahman, and gladden it for me. Join together kṣatra, and gladden it for me.”

*Translation note:* Sāyaṇa reads **brahman** here as the Brāhmaṇa social order and **kṣatra** as the Kṣatriya ruling order, in view of their juxtaposition. He also discusses broader meanings of *brahman*. We must not insert his chosen interpretation silently into the mūla translation.

### English translation of Sāyaṇa (first portion, draft)

“Beginning with ‘Brahma saṃdhattam’: In the Veda the word *brahman* is principally used of the supreme Self, the cause of the universe. Thus the Aitareyins teach in their Upaniṣad, ‘Consciousness is the foundation; consciousness is Brahman’; the Taittirīyas, ‘Brahman is truth, knowledge, and infinity’; and the Vājasaneyins, ‘Brahman is knowledge and bliss.’ ...”
