# तैत्तिरीयब्राह्मणम् १.१.१

## 0. Introduction: Why these mantras come first

### Sāyaṇa

यद्यपि संहिताया 'इषे तूर्जे त्वा' इत्यादीनां दर्शपूर्णमासमन्त्राणामादौ समाम्नातत्वात्तद्व्याख्यानरूपे ब्राह्मणे पौरोडाशिककाण्डमादौ पठितव्यं तथाऽपि दर्शपूर्णमासयोराहवनीयाद्यग्निसापेक्षत्वादग्नीनां चाऽऽधानसाध्यत्वात्प्रथमकाण्डस्याऽऽद्ये प्रपाठकेऽग्न्याधानविधिरुच्यते ।

यद्यपि 'कृत्तिकास्वग्निमादधीत' इत्ययमेवाऽऽधानब्राह्मणस्योपक्रमस्तथाऽपि संहितायां ग्रहकाण्डे शुक्रामन्थिग्रहचारार्थीनां मन्त्राणामनुक्तत्वेनात्र वक्तव्यत्वात्तेषां चाऽऽशासनीयफलप्रतिपादकत्वेन मङ्गलरूपत्वादादावनुवाके तान्परिशिष्टान्मन्त्रानभिदधानोऽर्थान्मङ्गलाचरणं संपादयति ।

### Sāyaṇa translation

Although the mantras of the new- and full-moon sacrifices, beginning with “Iṣe tvorje tvā,” are recited at the beginning of the Saṃhitā, and one might therefore expect the Pauroḍāśika section to appear first in the Brāhmaṇa that explains them, the Darśa and Pūrṇamāsa sacrifices require the Āhavanīya and the other sacred fires. Those fires must first be established by the rite of ādhāna. Accordingly, the first prapāṭhaka of the first kāṇḍa prescribes the establishment of the sacred fires.

Although “One should establish the fire under the Kṛttikās” is properly the beginning of the Brāhmaṇa on the establishment of the fires, the mantras used in the ritual handling of the Śukra and Manthin grahas have not been stated in the Graha section of the Saṃhitā and must therefore be supplied here. Moreover, since they express desirable blessings, they are auspicious. By placing these supplementary mantras in the opening anuvāka, the text indirectly performs an auspicious invocation.

## 1. Joining brahman, kṣatra, and the blessings

### Saṃhitā

ब्रह्म॒ संध॑त्तं॒ तन्मे॑ जिन्वतम् ।
क्ष॒त्रꣳ संध॑त्तं॒ तन्मे॑ जिन्वतम् ।
इष॒ꣳ॒ संध॑त्तं॒ तां मे॑ जिन्वतम् ।
ऊर्ज॒ꣳ॒ संध॑त्तं॒ तां मे॑ जिन्वतम् ।
र॒यिꣳ संध॑त्तं॒ तां मे॑ जिन्वतम् ।
पुष्टि॒ꣳ॒ संध॑त्तं॒ तां मे॑ जिन्वतम् ।
प्र॒जाꣳ संध॑त्तं॒ तां मे॑ जिन्वतम् ।
प॒शून्थ्संध॑त्तं॒ तान्मे॑ जिन्वतम् ।

### Mūla translation

Bring brahman together and make it flourish for me.
Bring kṣatra together and make it flourish for me.
Bring nourishment (iṣ) together and make it flourish for me.
Bring sustaining vigor (ūrj) together and make it flourish for me.
Bring wealth together and make it flourish for me.
Bring bodily prosperity together and make it flourish for me.
Bring offspring together and make them flourish for me.
Bring cattle together and make them flourish for me.

### Sāyaṇa

ब्रह्म संधत्तमित्यादिना । ब्रह्मशब्दो जगत्कारणे परमात्मनि मुख्यतया वेदे प्रयुज्यते । तथा चैतरेयिणः स्वकीयोपनिषदि समामनन्ति — 'प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठा प्रज्ञानं ब्रह्म' इति । तैत्तिरीयाश्च — 'सत्यं ज्ञानमनन्तं ब्रह्म' इति । वाजसनेयिनश्च — 'विज्ञानमानन्दं ब्रह्म' इति । एवं सति ब्रह्मशब्दस्य तत्प्रतिपादकवर्णमात्रपरत्वेऽपि परब्रह्मणः सहसा बुद्धिस्थत्वादर्थान्मङ्गलाचरणं संपद्यते । तस्य ब्रह्मणः प्रतिपादकत्वेन वेदोऽपि ब्रह्मशब्दस्यार्थः । ततो ग्रन्थादौ वेदपुरुषोऽप्यनुस्मृतो भवति । तादृशवेदाध्ययनाध्यापनयोर्मुख्याधिकारसंपादिका ब्राह्मणजातिरपि ब्रह्मशब्दस्यार्थः । सोऽयमत्र मन्त्रे विवक्षितः उत्तरवाक्यगतक्षत्रशब्दसाहचर्यात् । एवं सत्यत्र प्रतिपाद्यत्वेनाविवक्षितोऽपि परमात्मा सहसा प्रतीयमानो विघ्नपरिहारादिरूपं माङ्गल्यं संपादयति । यथा ब्राह्मणभोजनार्थमानीतस्य दधिकुम्भस्य दर्शनेन ग्रामान्तरे जिगमिषूणां माङ्गल्यं तद्वत् । तस्मादाद्यानुवाके 'ब्रह्म संधत्तम्' इत्यादि मन्त्राणामाम्नानं युक्ततरम् ।

मन्त्रविनियोगं चाऽऽपस्तम्बो दर्शयति — “अपरेणोत्तरवेदिं ग्रहावरत्नी वा संधत्तः — ब्रह्म संधत्तं तन्मे जिन्वतं क्षत्रं संधत्तं तन्मे जिन्वतम् ...” इति । अथाध्वर्युः शुक्रपात्रं प्रतिप्रस्थाता मन्थिपात्रं चाऽऽदायोत्तरवेदेः पश्चाद्भागेऽवस्थाय स्वहस्तस्थिते पात्रे परस्परसंस्पृष्टे कुरुतः । यद्वा स्वकीयावरत्नी परस्परसंस्पृष्टे कुरुतः । हे ग्रहौ युवां ब्रह्म यजमानसंबन्धिनीं ब्राह्मणजातिं संधत्तं स्वोचितधर्मराहित्यं यथा न भवति तथा संपादयतम् । किं च मे तद्ब्रह्म मदीयां ब्राह्मणजातिं जिन्वतं प्रीणयतम् । एवमग्रेऽपि सर्वत्र योज्यम् । क्षत्रं देशाधिपतिस्वामिगता क्षत्रियजातिः । इष इत्यन्नम् । ऊर्क्षीरादिरसः । रयिर्धनम् । पुष्टिः शरीरपोषः । प्रजा पुत्रपौत्रादिः । पशवो गवादयः ।

### Sāyaṇa translation

On the passage beginning “Bring brahman together”: The word *brahman* is primarily used in the Veda for the supreme Self, the cause of the universe. Thus the followers of the Aitareya recension declare in their Upaniṣad, “Consciousness is the foundation; consciousness is Brahman”; the Taittirīyas declare, “Brahman is truth, knowledge, and infinity”; and the Vājasaneyins, “Brahman is knowledge and bliss.” Even when the word *brahman* refers merely to the sounds that denote that supreme Brahman, the supreme Brahman immediately comes to mind, and thus an auspicious opening is indirectly accomplished.

Since the Veda makes that Brahman known, *brahman* can also signify the Veda. Thus the Veda, personified, is remembered at the beginning of the work. The word can also denote the Brāhmaṇa social order, whose members hold the primary entitlement to study and teach that Veda. This last meaning is intended in the present mantra, because *brahman* stands next to *kṣatra* in the following sentence. Nevertheless, even though the supreme Self is not the meaning directly intended here, the immediate recollection of it brings auspiciousness, including the removal of obstacles. This is like the auspicious sight, to persons setting out for another village, of a pot of curds being brought for feeding Brāhmaṇas. Therefore it is especially appropriate that the opening anuvāka should contain the mantras beginning “Bring brahman together.”

Āpastamba also indicates the ritual application of the mantras: “Behind the Uttara-vedi the two grahas, or the two forearms, are brought together, with the words ‘Bring brahman together and gladden it for me; bring kṣatra together and gladden it for me ...’”

The Adhvaryu takes the Śukra vessel and the Pratiprasthātṛ takes the Manthin vessel. Standing behind the Uttara-vedi, they bring the two vessels held in their hands into contact with each other. Alternatively, they bring their respective forearms into contact.

The sense of the address is: “O two grahas, bring together the Brāhmaṇa order belonging to the yajamāna, ensuring that it does not become devoid of its proper duties. And gladden, or please, that Brāhmaṇa order for me.” The same construction is to be applied to the following expressions. *Kṣatra* denotes the Kṣatriya order associated with ruling the country; *iṣ* means food; *ūrj* denotes nourishing substance such as milk; *rayi* is wealth; *puṣṭi* is bodily nourishment; *prajā* denotes children, grandchildren and other descendants; and *paśavaḥ* denotes cattle and other domestic animals.

#### Philology: The word brahman

Sāyaṇa distinguishes the primary theological meaning of *brahman*, the Veda as its verbal expression, and the Brāhmaṇa social order. He prefers the third meaning locally because of its pairing with *kṣatra*. The unqualified mūla translation retains the term *brahman* so that this interpretive choice remains visible.
