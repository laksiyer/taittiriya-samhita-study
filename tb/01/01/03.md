# तैत्तिरीयब्राह्मणम् १.१.३

This edition presents the full twelve recitation portions of TB 1.1.3, divided into short coherent units for study. The accented mūla is checked against the independent Sanskrit Documents transcription. Sāyaṇa's Sanskrit is transcribed from the user-provided OCR witness; **its readings still require collation against a printed edition**. The English follows the Sanskrit prose throughout, distinguishing the Brāhmaṇa's ritual claims from Sāyaṇa's explanations. Devanagari Sanskrit in this edition is for study and must not replace an authorized recitation text.

## 0. Introduction: materials for the ritual ground

### Sāyaṇa

.1.3

द्वितीयानुवाक आधानार्थे नक्षत्रादिकालोऽभिहितः। तृतीये देवयजनार्थाः संभारा उच्यन्ते। यदुक्तं सूत्रकारेण—‘प्राचीनप्रवणं देवयजनमुद्धत्य’ इति। तदिदं विधत्ते—

### Sāyaṇa translation

In the second anuvāka, the calendrical conditions, such as the nakṣatras, for establishing the sacred fires were set forth. In the third, the materials (*saṃbhāras*) required for the sacrificial ground (*devayajana*) are explained. As the Sūtra author says, “Having dug up a sacrificial ground sloping towards the east.” The Brāhmaṇa now prescribes the related action.

### Notes

#### Editorial: Text and ritual context

The source’s opening “.1.3” has been normalized only in our title. The Āpastamba citation awaits exact sūtra location.


## 1. Excavation and removal of what is unfit

### Saṃhitā

उद्ध॑न्ति । यदे॒वास्या॑ अमे॒ध्यम् । तदप॑ हन्ति ।

### Mūla translation

They dig up the ground. Whatever on it is ritually unfit, they thereby remove.

### Sāyaṇa

भूमेरूर्ध्वभागं खनित्रादिना खनेत्। तेन निष्ठीवनादिनिमित्तं यदमेध्यं तदपहतं भवति। उद्धननमन्त्रस्तु—“उद्धन्यमानम्” इत्यादि। स चोपरीतनपाठकाद्वाम्नातत्वात्तत्रैव व्याख्यास्यते। एवमुत्तरेष्वपि विधिषु द्रष्टव्यम्।

### Sāyaṇa translation

One should dig away the upper portion of the earth with a digging implement and the like. Thus anything made impure by spitting or similar causes is removed. The excavation formula begins “uddhanyamānam …”; since it is transmitted in a later prapāṭhaka, it will be explained there. The same applies to the mantras pertaining to subsequent operations.

### Notes

#### Editorial: Text and ritual context

The action is described here, while its recited mantra occurs elsewhere in the textual sequence.


## 2. Sprinkling water to pacify the ground

### Saṃhitā

अ॒पोऽवो᳚क्षति॒ शान्त्यै᳚ ।

### Mūla translation

He sprinkles water, for pacification.

### Sāyaṇa

उद्धननकृता भूमेर्वेदना जलेन शाम्यति। ‘आपो वै शान्ताः’ इत्यन्यत्रोपशमहेतुत्वश्रवणात्।

### Sāyaṇa translation

The pain occasioned to the earth by digging is soothed with water, because another passage declares “The waters are peaceful,” establishing their function as an agent of calming.

### Notes

#### Editorial: Text and ritual context

This is Sāyaṇa’s explanation of the Brāhmaṇa’s concise *śāntyai*.


## 3. Earthly saṃbhāra I: sand

### Saṃhitā

सिक॑ता॒ निव॑पति । ए॒तद्वा अ॒ग्नेर्वै᳚श्वान॒रस्य॑ रू॒पम् । रू॒पेणै॒व वै᳚श्वान॒रमव॑रुन्धे ।

### Mūla translation

He scatters sand. This indeed is the form of Agni Vaiśvānara. Through his form he secures Vaiśvānara.

### Sāyaṇa

सिकतानां तद्रूपत्वं ‘ वैश्वानरस्य रूपम् ’ इति मन्त्रादवगन्तव्यम्। सिकताद्यो द्रव्यविशेषा देवयजनभूमौ प्रक्षेपणीयाः संभाराः। अत एव सूत्रकारेणोक्तम्—‘ सप्त पार्थिवान्संभारानाहरत्येवं वानस्पत्यान् ’ इति। तत्र पार्थिवेषु सिकतारूप एकः संभार उक्तः।

### Sāyaṇa translation

That sand is Agni Vaiśvānara’s form is known from the mantra “the form of Vaiśvānara.” Sand and the other specified substances placed on the sacrificial ground are called *saṃbhāras*. Thus the Sūtra author says: “He brings seven earthly saṃbhāras, and an equal number of vegetal ones.” Sand is the first of the earthly group.

### Notes

#### Editorial: Text and ritual context

The Sūtra announces seven materials of each group, though this anuvāka treats only six distinct vegetal materials explicitly.


## 4. Earthly saṃbhāra II: saline earth (ūṣā)

### Saṃhitā

ऊषा॒न्निव॑पति । पुष्टि॒र्वा ए॒षा प्र॒जन॑नम् । यदूषाः᳚ । पुष्ट्या॑मे॒व प्र॒जन॑ने॒ऽग्निमाध॑त्ते । अथो॑ सं॒ज्ञान॑ ए॒व । सं॒ज्ञान॒ग्ग्॒ ह्ये॑तत्प॑शू॒नाम् । यदूषाः᳚ ।

### Mūla translation

He scatters *ūṣā*, saline earth. This is nourishment and generation. He thus establishes Agni amid nourishment and reproduction, and also at a place of recognition, for *ūṣā* is a place recognized by livestock.

### Sāyaṇa

पशवो ह्युपरक्षेत्रोत्पन्नं तृणादिकं रसान्तरयुक्तत्वादादरेण भक्षयित्वा पुष्टाः सन्तोऽपल्यानुत्पादयंति। तस्मादूषा लवणाम्भसा मिश्रा भूम्यवयवा इति यावत्। सैषा पुष्टिः प्रजननं च तस्मादूपनिवापेन तदुभयप्राप्तिः। अपि च

संज्ञाने प्रदेश एवाभिहितो भवति । एतचोषरक्षेत्रं पशूनां संज्ञानम् । पशवो घ्राणेन तत्संस्थानं जानन्ति । तस्मादूषा आधानस्य स्थाने निवपनीयाः ।

### Sāyaṇa translation

Animals eagerly eat plants arising in saline ground because of their distinctive taste, and thus become nourished and produce offspring. *Ūṣā* means portions of soil mixed with saline moisture. Hence its use brings nourishment and procreation. The saline tract is also a place known to livestock, which identify its location by smell. Thus *ūṣā* is to be scattered at the place of the fire’s establishment.

### Notes

#### Editorial: Text and ritual context

The translation “saline earth” follows Sāyaṇa’s explicit description, not an assumption about a modern chemical substance.


## 5. Heaven, earth and the dark mark on the moon

### Saṃhitā

द्यावा॑पृथि॒वी स॒हास्ता᳚म् । ते वि॑य॒ती अ॑ब्रूताम् । अस्त्वे॒व नौ॑ स॒ह य॒ज्ञिय॒मिति॑ । यद॒मुष्या॑ य॒ज्ञिय॒मासी᳚त् । तद॒स्याम॑दधात् । त ऊषा॑ अभवन् । यद॒स्या य॒ज्ञिय॒मासी᳚त् । तद॒मुष्या॑मदधात् । तद॒दश्च॒न्द्रम॑सि कृ॒ष्णम् । ऊषा᳚न्नि॒वप॑न्न॒दो ध्या॑येत् । द्यावा॑पृथि॒व्योरे॒व य॒ज्ञिये॒ऽग्निमाध॑त्ते ।

### Mūla translation

Heaven and earth were once together. As they separated, they said: “May what is fit for sacrifice remain joined between us.” What was sacrificial in heaven was placed in earth, becoming the *ūṣā* deposits. What was sacrificial in earth was placed in heaven, becoming that dark mark visible on the moon. While scattering *ūṣā* he should meditate upon that [dark mark]. He thereby establishes Agni in what is sacrificial in both heaven and earth.

### Sāyaṇa

एतन्निवपनकाले ध्यानविशेषं विधत्ते—

सृष्टिकालै द्यावापृथिव्यौ मध्यगतान्तरिक्षव्यवधानरहिते अभूतां तदा प्राणिनामवकाशार्धमीश्वरानुज्ञया परस्परवियोगं गते वियोगकाले च स्नेहातिशयात्परस्परमेवमब्रूतामावयोर्यज्ञयोग्यं यत्सारमस्ति तत्सथैव तिष्ठतु । भूसारस्य दिवो वियोगो मा भूद्भूसारस्य च भूमेर्वियोगो मा भूदिति । ततोऽमुष्या दिवः संबन्धि यद्यज्ञियं सारं तदस्यां पृथिव्यां सा द्यौरस्थापयत् । ते च द्युलोकसारांशा एते दृश्यमाना भूम्यवयवा ऊषरूपा अभवन् । तस्यां पृथिव्यां यद्यज्ञियं सारमासीत्तदमुष्यां दिवि भूमिरस्थापयत् । तच्च स्थापितं भूसारमदो दृश्यमानं चन्द्रमसि कलङ्करूपं कृष्णम् । तस्माद्भूसारत्वादूपाणां निवपनकाले तत्कृष्णरूपमत्र तिष्ठतीति ध्यायेत् । तथा सति द्युलोकसंबन्धिन्यूपे भूलोकसंबन्धिनि कृष्णे चाग्निराहितो भवति ।

### Sāyaṇa translation

At creation heaven and earth were not yet parted by intervening space. When separated to provide room for beings, they spoke out of mutual affection, wishing their sacrificial essences not to be severed. Heaven placed its sacrificial essence in earth, where it became the visible saline deposits. Earth placed its sacrificial essence above, where it appears as the dark mark upon the moon. Thus while depositing *ūṣā* one should contemplate that dark lunar form as present here. Through this meditation the fire is established in the sacrificial essence of both worlds.

### Notes

#### Editorial: Text and ritual context

The explanation is a Vedic etiological and ritual meditation, not a physical explanation of lunar surface features.


## 6. Earthly saṃbhāra III: soil thrown up by a mouse

### Saṃhitā

अ॒ग्निर्दे॒वेभ्यो॒ निला॑यत । आ॒खू रू॒पं कृ॒त्वा । स पृ॑थि॒वीं प्रावि॑शत् । स ऊ॒तीः कु॑र्वा॒णः पृ॑थि॒वीमनु॒ सम॑चरत् । तदा॑खुकरी॒षम॑भवत् । यदा॑खुकरी॒षꣳ सं॑भा॒रो भव॑ति । यदे॒वास्य॒ तत्र॒ न्य॑क्तम् । तदे॒वाव॑रुन्धे ।

### Mūla translation

Agni hid himself from the gods, taking the form of a mouse. He entered the earth and moved about inside it making burrows. That became the mouse’s *karīṣa*. When this is included among the materials, one secures precisely the portion of Agni deposited there.

### Sāyaṇa

अग्निः कदाचित्केनापि निमित्तेन देवेभ्योऽपरक्तः सन्मूषको भूत्वा तदीयं रूपं च संपूर्ण कृत्वा निलीनो देवैरदृश्यो भूत्वा स च पृथिवीं प्रविष्टो बिलानि कुर्वार्णोऽनुक्रमेण भूमेरन्तरेव समचरत् । यद्विलनिर्माणेषु निःसारितं पांसुरूपं तदेतदाखुकरीपं तस्मिन्संहृते सति तत्र निगूढं यदग्नेस्तेजस्तत्सर्वं प्राप्नोति ।

### Sāyaṇa translation

For some reason Agni withdrew from the gods, assumed the entire form of a mouse, and hid from their sight. Moving through the earth by making holes, he threw out soil from the burrows. This excavated dust or earth is called *ākhukarīṣa*. By collecting it, the sacrificer gains the fiery potency that Agni left concealed there.

### Notes

#### Editorial: Text and ritual context

Sāyaṇa specifically identifies *karīṣa* here with soil excavated from burrows, rather than simply translating it as animal dung.


## 7. Earthly saṃbhāra IV: earth from a termite mound

### Saṃhitā

ऊर्जं॒ वा ए॒तꣳ रसं॑ पृथि॒व्या उ॑प॒दीका॒ उद्दि॑हन्ति । यद्व॒ल्मीक᳚म् । यद्व॑ल्मीकव॒पा सं॑भा॒रो भव॑ति । ऊर्ज॑मे॒व रसं॑ पृथि॒व्या अव॑रुन्धे । अथो॒ श्रोत्र॑मे॒व । श्रोत्र॒ग्ग्॒ ह्ये॑तत्पृ॑थि॒व्याः । यद्व॒ल्मीकः॑ । अब॑धिरो भवति । य ए॒वं वेद॑ ।

### Mūla translation

The *upadīkā* creatures bring up this nourishing sap of earth as a mound. When earth from the mound is a material, he obtains the nourishing essence of earth, and hearing also, for the mound is earth’s ear. One who knows this becomes free of deafness.

### Sāyaṇa

उपदीकाः पिपीलिकासमाना आर्द्रकृतमृत्तिकोपचयक्षमा जन्तवस्ते हि पृथिव्याः संबन्धिनमूर्क्षाब्दाभिधेयं रसं पृथिव्याः सकाशादूर्ध्वमानीयोपचिन्वन्ति तत्संभारेण भूसारं प्राप्नोति । अपि च श्रोत्रेन्द्रियसामर्थ्यमपि प्राप्नोति । वल्मीकस्य पृथिवीश्रोत्रत्त्वात् ।

एतद्वेदनं प्रशंसति—

### Sāyaṇa translation

The *upadīkās* are small creatures resembling ants, capable of building up moist earth. They convey and accumulate the nutritive essence of earth. Thus collecting mound-earth secures the earth’s nutritive sap. It also secures hearing because the mound is figuratively the earth’s ear. Hence the Brāhmaṇa praises knowledge of the rite with the claim that such a knower does not become deaf.

### Notes

#### Editorial: Text and ritual context

Retain *upadīkā* rather than asserting an exact modern biological identification; ant-like mound-builders are Sāyaṇa’s description.


## 8. Earthly saṃbhāra V: fertile mud (sūda)

### Saṃhitā

प्र॒जाप॑तिः प्र॒जा अ॑सृजत । तासा॒मन्न॒मुपा᳚क्षीयत । ताभ्यः॒ सूद॒मुप॒ प्राभि॑नत् । ततो॒ वै तासा॒मन्नं॒ नाक्षी॑यत । यस्य॒ सूदः॑ संभा॒रो भव॑ति । नास्य॑ गृ॒हेऽन्नं॑ क्षीयते ।

### Mūla translation

Prajāpati created creatures, but their food began to run out. He distributed *sūda* for them, whereupon their food ceased to run out. Whoever includes *sūda* among his materials finds that food does not fail in his house.

### Sāyaṇa

अथ पञ्चमं संभारं विधत्ते—

प्रजापतिः प्रजा असृजत । तासामन्नमुपो-

ताभ्य॒स्सूद॒मुप॒प्राभि॑नत् । ततो॒ वै तासा॒मन्न॒न्नाख्षी॑यत । यस्य॒ सूद॑स्सम्भा॒रो भव॑ति । नास्य॑ गृ॒हेऽन्न॑ङ्ख्षीयते, इति।

पुरा कदाचित्मजापतिः सृष्टानां प्रजानां यदन्नं तद्भुज्यमानमुपक्षीणमभूत् । ततः प्रजाथ सूदमशोष्यजलप्रदेशावस्थितं कद्दममुपाानीय प्राभिनत् । तेषु तेषु पृथ्वीप्रदेशेषु पृथक्पृथक्प्रसारितवान् । ततस्तत्र तत्र सस्योत्पत्तौ सत्यां तासां प्रजानामननमक्षीणमभूत् । अतस्तत्संभारेण गृहेऽन्नमक्षयं भवति ।

### Sāyaṇa translation

Long ago Prajāpati saw that the food of the creatures he had created was diminishing through consumption. He brought *sūda*, mud from a place where water had not dried up, and spread it separately over different tracts of land. Crops then grew there and the creatures’ food was no longer exhausted. Hence gathering this material is said to ensure an unfailing supply of food in the sacrificer’s household.

### Notes

#### Editorial: Text and ritual context

The uploaded witness contains the word उपो- split across a line; the independent mūla has उपाक्षीयत. This edition presents the independent reading.


## 9. Earthly saṃbhāra VI: the boar, earth and pebbles

### Saṃhitā

आपो॒ वा इ॒दमग्रे॑ सलि॒लमा॑सीत् । तेन॑ प्र॒जाप॑तिरश्राम्यत् । क॒थमि॒द२ꣳ स्या॒दिति॑ । सो॑ऽपश्यत्पुष्करप॒र्णं तिष्ठ॑त् । सो॑ऽमन्यत । अस्ति॒ वै तत् । यस्मि॑न्नि॒दमधि॒तिष्ठ॒तीति॑ । स व॑रा॒हो रू॒पं कृ॒त्वोप॒ न्य॑मज्जत् । स पृ॑थि॒वीम॒ध आ᳚र्च्छत् । तस्या॑ उप॒हत्योद॑मज्जत् । तत्पु॑ष्करप॒र्णे᳚ऽप्रथयत् । यदप्र॑थयत् । तत्पृ॑थि॒व्यै पृ॑थिवि॒त्वम् । अभू॒द्वा इ॒दमिति॑ । तद्भूम्यै॑ भूमि॒त्वम् । तां दिशोऽनु॒ वातः॒ सम॑वहत् । ताꣳ शर्क॑राभिरदृꣳहत् । शं वै नो॑ऽभू॒दिति॑ । तच्छर्क॑राणाꣳ शर्कर॒त्वम् । यद्व॑रा॒हवि॑हतꣳ संभा॒रो भव॑ति । अ॒स्यामे॒वाछ॑म्बट्कारम॒ग्निमाध॑त्ते । शर्क॑रा भवन्ति॒ धृत्यै᳚ । अथो॑ शं॒त्वाय॑ ।

### Mūla translation

At first this world was water, a flood. Prajāpati laboured over how the world might come to be. He saw a lotus leaf resting [on the waters] and thought that there must be something supporting it. Taking the form of a boar, he dived, reached earth beneath, brought up a portion of it and spread it upon the lotus leaf. Because it was spread (*prath*), it was called *pṛthivī*, and because “it has come to be” (*abhūt*), it was called *bhūmi*. Wind moved over it in every direction, and it was made firm with pebbles. Those pebbles received their name from the declaration “It has become auspicious for us” (*śam*). When soil disturbed by a boar is among the materials, the sacrificer establishes Agni securely on earth. Pebbles are used for firmness and well-being.

### Sāyaṇa

अथ षष्ठं संभारं विधत्ते—

आपो वा इदमग्रे सलिलमासीत् । तेन प्रजा-

यद्व॑रा॒हवि॑हत सम्भा॒रो भव॑ति । अ॒स्यामे॒वाछ॑म्बट्कारम॒ग्निमाध॑त्ते । शर्क॑रा भवन्ति॒ धृत्यै । अथो॑ श॒न्त्वाय॑, इति।

### Sāyaṇa translation

In the beginning the world of mountains, rivers, plants and creatures was unmanifest, appearing only as *salila*, water or the undifferentiated cause. Sāyaṇa discusses the etymology of *salila* and an alternative identification with the worlds, then describes Prajāpati’s deliberation as a kind of contemplative exertion. Noticing a lotus leaf above the water, Prajāpati inferred an underlying support. Becoming a boar, he dived beside the lotus stalk, found the earth below, lifted a portion of wet soil with his tusk, and spread it on the leaf. Its spreading explains the name *pṛthivī*; its coming into existence explains *bhūmi*. Wind dried it, and small stones made it firm. Sāyaṇa also derives *śarkarā* by a play upon *śam*, happiness or welfare. Because the boar-recovered earth bears this creative power, soil disturbed or dug by a boar is ritually collected. Pebbles are placed for stability and auspiciousness.

#### Editorial: Text and ritual context

## 10. Earthly saṃbhāra VII: gold and Agni’s seed

### Saṃhitā

सरे॑ता अ॒ग्निरा॒धेय॒ इत्या॑हुः । आपो॒ वरु॑णस्य॒ पत्न॑य आसन् । ता अ॒ग्निर॒भ्य॑ध्यायत् । ताः सम॑भवत् । तस्य॒ रेतः॒ परा॑ऽपतत् । तद्धिर॑ण्यमभवत् । यद्धिर॑ण्यमु॒पास्य॑ति । सरे॑तसमे॒वाग्निमाध॑त्ते ।

### Mūla translation

They say that Agni should be established with seed. The waters were Varuṇa’s wives. Agni desired them and united with them. His seed fell away and became gold. By placing gold he establishes Agni possessed of seed.

### Sāyaṇa

अथ सप्तमं संभारं विधत्ते—

सरे॑ता अ॒ग्निरा॒धेय॒ इत्या॑हुः । आपो॒ वरु॑णस्य॒ पत्न॑य आसन्न् । ता अ॒ग्निर॒भ्य॑ध्यायत् । तास्सम॑भवत् । तस्य॒ रेत॒ परा॑ऽपतत् । तद्धिर॑ण्यमभवत् । यद्धिर॑ण्यमु॒पास्य॑ति । सरे॑तसमे॒वाग्निमाध॑त्ते, इति।

या अब्देवता वरुणस्य भार्यास्ताः कामुकोऽग्निध्यैत्वा संभोगं कृतवान्‌ । तदानीं व्यग्रस्य तस्य रेतो भूमौ पतित्वा हिरण्यमभूत्‌ । अतोऽत्र हिरण्ये प्रक्षिप्ते सति सरेतस्कोऽग्निराहितो भवति ।

### Sāyaṇa translation

The water-deities were the wives of Varuṇa. Agni, desiring them, united with them; at that moment his seed fell onto the earth and became gold. By placing gold among the materials the sacrificer establishes Agni as a being endowed with seed, and therefore with generative potency.

## 11. Placement of gold on the northern side

### Saṃhitā

पुरु॑ष॒ इन्न्वै स्वाद्रेत॑सो बीभथ्सत॒ इत्या॑हुः । उ॒त्त॒र॒त उपा᳚स्य॒त्यबी॑भथ्सायै ।

### Mūla translation

They say that a person recoils from his own seed. He places [the gold] to the north, to avoid revulsion.

### Sāyaṇa

एतस्य हिरण्यस्य प्रक्षेपेस्थानं विधत्ते--

पुरु॑ष॒ इन्न्वै स्वाद्रेत॑सो बीभथ्सत॒ इत्या॑हुः । उ॒त्त॒र॒त उपास्य॒त्यबी॑भथ्सायै, इति।

इङ्वा इति निपातत्रयं मिलित्वा प्रसिद्धिमाचष्टे । लोके सर्वोऽपि पुरुषः स्वकीयं रेतो हस्तेन स्पृष्टुं जुगुप्सते । तदेतत्प्रसिद्धमित्याभिज्ञा आहुः । तस्माद्वाग्निस्थापनप्रदेशं विहायोत्तरस्मिन्पार्श्वे हिरण्यं प्रक्षिपेत् । तचाबीत्सयै संपद्यते । यदुक्तं सूत्रकारेण---"द्वेष्याय रजतं प्रयच्छति" इति ।

### Sāyaṇa translation

Sāyaṇa explains the particles *it nu vai* as indicating a well-known fact: a person is reluctant to touch his own semen. Thus the gold is deposited not in the very place where Agni is established but to the northern side, so that the ritual act is free from revulsion. He then cites a Sūtra prescription concerning silver being given to someone regarded as an adversary.

### Notes

#### Editorial: Text and ritual context

The silver-related prescription belongs with the next unit and must not be confused with the northern placement of gold.


## 12. Silver and the transfer of distress

### Saṃhitā

अति॒ प्रय॑च्छति । आर्ति॑मे॒वाति॒प्रय॑च्छति ।

### Mūla translation

He hands [it] beyond. He thereby hands distress away from himself.

### Sāyaṇa

तदिदं विधत्ते---

अति॒प्रय॑च्छति । आर्ति॑मे॒वाति॒प्रय॑च्छति, इति।

देवयजने भक्षिप्तं सुवर्णमतिक्रम्यान्यद्रजतं प्रयच्छेत । तथा सति स्वात्मानमतिक्रम्यान्यस्मै द्वेष्याय स्वकीयामार्तिं दत्तवान्भवति । तदेवं पार्थिवाः संभारा विहिताः ।

### Sāyaṇa translation

One should hand over a separate piece of silver beyond the gold already placed on the sacrificial ground. In Sāyaṇa’s interpretation this amounts to passing the sacrificer’s distress away from himself to another, specifically a person regarded as an adversary. This completes his explanation of the earthly *saṃbhāras*.

#### Editorial: Text and ritual context

This is a ritual attribution in the source, not a general recommendation for conduct toward others.


## 13. Vegetal saṃbhāra I: aśvattha wood

### Saṃhitā

अ॒ग्निर्दे॒वेभ्यो॒ निला॑यत । अश्वो॑ रू॒पं कृ॒त्वा । सो᳚ऽश्व॒त्थे सं॑वथ्स॒रम॑तिष्ठत् । तद॑श्व॒त्थस्या᳚श्वत्थ॒त्वम् । यदाश्व॑त्थः संभा॒रो भव॑ति । यदे॒वास्य॒ तत्र॒ न्य॑क्तम् । तदे॒वाव॑रुन्धे ।

### Mūla translation

Agni hid from the gods, taking the form of a horse. He dwelt in the aśvattha tree for a year, whence the name *aśvattha*. When wood of aśvattha is a material, one secures what of Agni was lodged there.

### Sāyaṇa

अथ वानस्पत्येषु सप्तसु संभारेषु प्रथमं संभारं विधत्ते---

अ॒ग्निर्दे॒वेभ्यो॒ निला॑यत । अश्वो॑ रू॒पङ्कृ॒त्वा । सोऽश्व॒त्थे सँ॑व्वथ्स॒रम॑तिष्ठत् । तद॑श्व॒त्थस्याश्वत्थ॒त्वम् । यदाश्व॑त्थस्सम्भा॒रो भव॑ति । यदे॒वास्य॒ तत्र॒ न्य॑क्तम् । तदे॒वाव॑रुन्धे, इति।

कदाचिदग्निर्देवेभ्योऽपक्रम्याश्वो भूत्वा तदीयं रूपं स्वस्य कृत्वाऽश्वत्थवृक्षे निरूढः संवत्सरमात्रमतिष्ठत् । अश्वस्तिष्ठत्यस्मिन्निति व्युत्पत्या तस्य वृक्षस्याश्वत्थनाम संपन्नम् । तस्मादश्वत्थसंमारेण तत्र निरूढमग्नेस्तेजः प्राप्नोति ।

### Sāyaṇa translation

Sāyaṇa states that Agni once withdrew from the gods and became a horse, taking on its complete form. He remained lodged in an aśvattha tree for a full year. Sāyaṇa explains the tree’s name as “where a horse stands” (*aśvas tiṣṭhati*). Through collecting aśvattha wood one gains the fiery potency that Agni left in the tree.

## 14. Vegetal saṃbhāra II: udumbara

### Saṃhitā

दे॒वा वा ऊर्जं॒ व्य॑भजन्त । तत॑ उदु॒म्बर॒ उद॑तिष्ठत् । ऊर्ग्वा उ॑दु॒म्बरः॑ । यदौदु॑म्बरः संभा॒रो भव॑ति । ऊर्ज॑मे॒वाव॑रुन्धे ।

### Mūla translation

The gods divided the nourishing essence, and from it the udumbara arose. The udumbara is nourishment. Whoever collects udumbara material obtains nourishment.

### Sāyaṇa

द्वितीयं संभारं विधत्ते---

दे॒वा वा ऊर्जँ॒व्व्य॑भजन्त । तत॑ उदु॒म्बर॒ उद॑तिष्ठत् । ऊर्ग्वा उ॑दु॒म्बरः॑ । यदौदु॑म्बरस्सम्भा॒रो भव॑ति । ऊर्ज॑मे॒वाव॑रुन्धे, इति।

देवाः सर्वे कदाचिदेकत्रोपविश्यान्नरसं विभक्तवन्तस्तदा तस्मिन्स्थाने पतितादन्नरसादुदुम्बरवृक्ष उदतिष्ठत् । तस्मादन्नरसस्यैवोदुम्वरत्वात्तत्संभारेणान्नरसं प्राप्नोत्येव ।

### Sāyaṇa translation

The gods sat together and apportioned the nourishing essence of food. From the essence that fell at that place arose the udumbara tree. Because the tree originated from food’s nourishment, gathering its wood gives the sacrificer that nourishing essence.

### Notes

#### Editorial: Text and ritual context

The text associates *udumbara* with *ūrj*, nourishing strength.


## 15. Vegetal saṃbhāra III: parṇa and the Soma leaf

### Saṃhitā

तृ॒तीय॑स्यामि॒तो दि॒वि सोम॑ आसीत् । तं गा॑य॒त्र्याह॑रत् । तस्य॑ प॒र्णम॑च्छिद्यत । तत्प॒र्णो॑ऽभवत् । तत्प॒र्णस्य॑ पर्ण॒त्वम् । यस्य॑ पर्ण॒मयः॑ संभा॒रो भव॑ति । सो॒म॒पी॒थमे॒वाव॑रुन्धे ।

### Mūla translation

Soma was in the third heaven from here. Gāyatrī brought him. A leaf was torn from him, and it became the parṇa tree, which thus received its name. Whoever includes parṇa wood among the materials secures the drinking of Soma.

### Sāyaṇa

अथ तृतीयं संभारं विधत्ते—

तृतीयस्यामितो दिवि सोम आसीत् । तं गायत्र्याहरत् । तस्य॑ प॒र्णम॑च्छिद्यत । तत्प॒र्णो॑ऽभवत् । तत्प॒र्णस्य॑ पर्ण॒त्वम् । यस्य॑ पर्ण॒मय॑स्सम्भा॒रो भव॑ति । सो॒म॒पी॒थमे॒वाव॑रुन्धे, इति।

इतो भूलोकादारभ्य गणनायामन्तरिक्षमतिक्रम्य परतो वर्तमाना या द्यौस्तृतीया तस्यां सोमवल्ली स्थिता । तं च सोमं गायत्री देवता तत्रत्यैः सोम- रक्षकैः सह युद्धं कृत्वा समाहरत् । अयं च वृत्तान्तः “कद्रूश्च वै सुपर्णा च” इत्यनुवाके प्रपञ्चितः । सहसा समानीयमानस्य तस्य सोमस्यैकं पर्णं छिन्नं सद्भूतौ पतित्वा पलाशवृक्षोऽभवत् । पर्णजन्यत्वात्तस्य वृक्षस्य पर्णनाम संप- न्नम् । सोमजन्यस्य तस्य संभरणेन सोमपानमेव प्राप्नोति ।

### Sāyaṇa translation

Counting from the earth and passing beyond the intermediate region, Soma was in the third heavenly world. The goddess Gāyatrī fetched him after struggling with his guardians. Sāyaṇa refers to another anuvāka beginning “Kadrū and Suparṇā” for the fuller narrative. As Soma was carried rapidly, a leaf separated, fell to earth, and became the palāśa tree called *parṇa*. Because the wood arose from Soma, incorporating it in the rite secures participation in Soma-drinking.

## 16. Parṇa as the hearer of brahman

### Saṃhitā

दे॒वा वै ब्रह्म॑न्नवदन्त । तत्प॒र्ण उपा॑श‍ृणोत् । सु॒श्रवा॒ वै नाम॑ । यत्प॑र्ण॒मयः॑ संभा॒रो भव॑ति । ब्र॒ह्म॒व॒र्च॒समे॒वाव॑रुन्धे ।

### Mūla translation

The gods spoke about *brahman*, and parṇa overheard. It is therefore famed as “the good hearer.” Whoever includes parṇa wood secures *brahmavarcas*, the radiance of sacred learning.

### Sāyaṇa

पुनरपि तमेव संभारं प्रशंसति—

दे॒वा वै ब्रह्म॑न्नवदन्त । तत्प॒र्ण उपा॑शृणोत् । सु॒श्रवा॒ वै नाम॑ । यत्प॑र्ण॒मय॑स्सम्भा॒रो भव॑ति । ब्र॒ह्म॒व॒र्च॒समे॒वाव॑रुन्धे, इति।

देवाः कदाचिदेकान्ते पलाशवृक्षच्छायायामुपविश्य परस्मिन्ब्रह्मणि संवादं कृतवन्तः । तत्सर्वे समीपे स्थितः पलाशवृक्षोऽशृणोत् । शोभनं वस्तु श्रुतवानिति व्युत्पत्त्या सुश्रवा इति नाम संपन्नम् । तस्मात्परं ब्रह्म श्रुतवतो वृक्षस्य संभरणेन ब्रह्मवर्चसमेव प्राप्नोति।

### Sāyaṇa translation

Once the gods sat privately beneath a palāśa tree and discussed the highest *brahman*. The tree standing nearby heard their whole discussion. As it heard what is excellent, it became renowned as *suśravas*, one who hears well. Through gathering wood from a tree that heard *brahman*, the sacrificer acquires *brahmavarcas*.

### Notes

#### Editorial: Text and ritual context

Sāyaṇa explicitly interprets *brahman* here in the sense of supreme Brahman; retain that as his exegetical choice.


## 17. Vegetal saṃbhāra IV: śamī as pacifier

### Saṃhitā

प्र॒जाप॑तिर॒ग्निम॑सृजत । सो॑ऽबिभे॒त्प्र मा॑ धक्ष्य॒तीति॑ । तꣳ श॒म्या॑ऽशमयत् । तच्छ॒म्यै॑ शमि॒त्वम् । यच्छ॑मी॒मयः॑ संभा॒रो भव॑ति । शान्त्या॒ अप्र॑दाहाय ।

### Mūla translation

Prajāpati created Agni and feared: “He will burn me.” He pacified him with śamī, from which comes the name *śamī*. When śamī wood is among the materials, it brings pacification and freedom from burning.

### Sāyaṇa

अथ चतुर्थं संभारं विधत्ते—

प्रजापतिरग्निमसृजत । सोऽबिभेन्मां धक्ष्यतीति । त श॒म्या॑ऽशमयत् । तच्छ॒म्यै॑ शमि॒त्वम् । यच्छ॑मी॒मय॑स्सम्भा॒रो भव॑ति । शान्त्या॒ अप्र॑दाहाय, इति।

प्रजापतिरग्निं सृष्ट्वा मामयमग्निः प्रधक्ष्यतीति भीतः शमीशाखया ज्वालामशमयत् । शमयत्यनेनेति व्युत्पत्त्या शमीति नाम संपन्नम् । अतस्तत्संभारः पूर्व विद्यमानस्य दाहस्योपशान्त्यै, इतः परमदाहाय च संपद्यते ।

### Sāyaṇa translation

After creating Agni, Prajāpati became afraid that the fire would consume him. He subdued the flames with a śamī branch. The tree was called śamī because it serves to *śamayati*, to pacify. Its wood accordingly removes existing burning and protects against future burning.

### Notes

#### Editorial: Text and ritual context

The language of protection is the Brāhmaṇa’s ritual rationale.


## 18. Vegetal saṃbhāra V: vikaṅkata and brilliance

### Saṃhitā

अ॒ग्नेः सृ॒ष्टस्य॑ य॒तः । विक॑ङ्कतं॒ भा आ᳚र्च्छत् । यद्वैक॑ङ्कतः संभा॒रो भव॑ति । भा ए॒वाव॑रुन्धे ।

### Mūla translation

As Agni, newly created, went forth, his brilliance reached the vikaṅkata tree. When vikaṅkata wood is included, one secures that radiance.

### Sāyaṇa

अथ पञ्चमं संभारं विधत्ते—

अ॒ग्नेस्सृ॒ष्टस्य॑ य॒तः । विक॑ङ्कत॒म्भा आर्च्छत् । यद्वैक॑ङ्कतस्सम्भा॒रो भव॑ति । भा ए॒वाव॑रुन्धे, इति।

प्रजापतिकर सृष्टोऽग्निस्त्वरया गच्छन्विकङ्कतवृक्षमपश्यत्तदा तदीयं तेजो वृक्षं प्राप्नोत् । अतस्तत्संभारे सति भा एव दीप्तिमेवावरुन्धे ।

### Sāyaṇa translation

When Agni, created by Prajāpati, moved swiftly, his splendor reached the vikaṅkata tree. By incorporating vikaṅkata wood, the sacrificer acquires that brilliance (*bhā*).

## 19. Vegetal saṃbhāra VI: lightning-struck wood and Agni’s heart

### Saṃhitā

सहृ॑दयो॒ऽग्निरा॒धेय॒ इत्या॑हुः । म॒रुतो॒ऽद्भिर॒ग्निम॑तमयन् । तस्य॑ ता॒न्तस्य॒ हृद॑य॒माच्छि॑न्दन् । साऽशनि॑रभवत् । यद॒शनि॑हतस्य वृ॒क्षस्य॑ संभा॒रो भव॑ति । सहृ॑दयमे॒वाग्निमाध॑त्ते ।

### Mūla translation

They say that Agni should be established with a heart. The Maruts wearied Agni with water; when he was exhausted they tore out his heart, which became lightning. When wood from a lightning-struck tree is a material, one establishes Agni together with his heart.

### Sāyaṇa

अथ षष्ठं संभारं विधत्ते—

सहृ॑दयो॒ऽग्निरा॒धेय॒ इत्या॑हुः । म॒रुतो॒ऽद्भिर॒ग्निम॑तमयन्न् । तस्य॑ ता॒न्तस्य॒ हृद॑य॒माच्छि॑न्दन्न् । साऽशनि॑रभवत् । यद॒शनि॑हतस्य वृ॒ख्षस्य॑ सम्भा॒रो भव॑ति । सहृ॑दयमे॒वाग्निमाध॑त्ते, इति।

मरुतः कदाचिदग्निना सह विरोधे सति तमग्निमद्भिरतमयन्ग्लांचं मापयन् । ततो निष्कुम्भस्याग्रेर्हृदयमाच्छिन्दन् । तस्य हृदयमशनिरूपेणोदभूत् । तस्मा-दशनिहतस्य वृक्षस्य संभारेण सहदयोऽग्निराहितो भवति ॥

### Sāyaṇa translation

The Maruts once came into conflict with Agni and exhausted him by means of water. When he was weakened, they tore out his heart, and it took the form of lightning. Therefore gathering material from a tree struck by lightning brings back Agni’s heart to the fire that is being established.
