# Taittirīya Saṃhitā 3.3.4

## अपां गुह्यनामधेयम्

### Saṃhitā

ए॒तद्वा अ॒पां ना॑म॒धेयं॒ गुह्यं॒ यदा॑धा॒वा मान्दा॑सु ते शुक्र शु॒क्रमा धू॑नो॒मीत्या॑हा॒पामे॒व ना॑म॒धेये॑न॒ गुह्ये॑न दि॒वो वृष्टि॒मव॑ रुंधे ।

### Mūla Translation

These are indeed the secret names of the Waters. By the secret name of the Waters he secures rain from heaven.

### Sāyaṇa

एतदेव मान्दा इत्यादिपदजातमपां गोप्यं नामधेयम् । लोके प्रसिद्ध्यभावाद्वैदिकमन्त्र एव प्रतीयमानतया गोप्यत्वम् ।

तैर्गुह्यनामभिर्देवताः परितोष्य दिवः सकाशाद्वृष्टिं संपादयति ।

### Sāyaṇa Translation

The series beginning with Māndā constitutes the hidden nomenclature of the Waters. They are secret because they are not known through ordinary worldly usage and are apprehended only from Vedic mantra.

By pleasing the presiding deities with these secret names, the sacrificer brings about rain from heaven.

## रात्रिरह्नो रूपम्

### Saṃhitā

शु॒क्रं ते॑ शु॒क्रेण॑ गृह्णा॒मीत्या॑है॒तद्वा अह्नो॑ रू॒पं यद्रात्रिः॒ सूर्य॑स्य र॒श्मयो॒ वृष्ट्या॑ ईशते ।

### Mūla Translation

He says, “Your shining essence I take with a shining essence.” Night is indeed the form of day. The rays of the Sun have lordship over rain.

### Sāyaṇa

रात्रिरिति यदेतदेवाह्नो रूपम्, अहर्निरूप्यते येन तदह्नो रूपं निरूपकमित्यर्थः । एतावदहर्नातोऽधिकमित्यह्न इयत्ता रात्र्या निरूप्यते ।

सेयं रात्रिः सूर्यस्य रश्मयश्च वृष्टिस्वामिभूताः ।

### Sāyaṇa Translation

Night is called the “form” of day because it is that by which the day is defined. It functions as the delimiter of the day. The extent of the day, “it reaches this far and no farther,” is determined by night.

Night and the rays of the Sun are both connected with the governance of rain.

### Notes

#### Study note: रूप

Sāyaṇa's explanation is निरूपकम्, a defining or delimiting condition, not merely visible form.

## ककुहं रूपं = वृष्टिः

### Saṃhitā

ककु॒हꣳ रू॒पं वृ॑ष॒भस्य॑ रोचते बृ॒हदित्या॑है॒तद्वा अ॑स्य ककु॒हꣳ रू॒पं यद्वृष्टी॑ रू॒पेणै॒व वृष्टि॒मव॑ रुंधे ।

### Mūla Translation

He says, “The prominent form of the Bull shines forth, great.” His prominent form is rain. By that form he secures rain.

### Sāyaṇa

हरणमन्त्रे प्रधानरूपवाचिभ्यां ककुहं रूपमिति पदाभ्यां वृष्टिर्विवक्षितेति दर्शयति ।

### Sāyaṇa Translation

The words kakuha and rūpa indicate a principal manifestation. The Brāhmaṇa makes clear that the manifestation intended is rain.

## हविषा हविर्यजति

### Saṃhitā

यत्ते॑ सोमादा᳚भ्यं॒ नाम॒ जागृ॑वीत्याह । ए॒तद्वै सोम॑स्यादाभ्यं॒ नाम॒ हविषा॒ हवि॑र्यजति ।

### Mūla Translation

He says, “That name of yours, O Soma, which is Adābhya, ever wakeful.” This indeed is Soma's name Adābhya. He sacrifices havis by means of havis.

### Sāyaṇa

यो यजमानोऽदाभ्यनामकं दधिग्रहं गृहीत्वा सोमाय देवाय जुहोति, स एव यजमानो हविषा दध्ना हविःस्वरूपं देवमुद्दिश्य यजतीत्युक्तम् ।

### Sāyaṇa Translation

The sacrificer takes the curd-graha called Adābhya and offers it to Soma. Thus “he sacrifices havis by means of havis” means that with curd, itself a havis, he worships Soma, here treated as havis-nature.

## अंशुग्रहः: प्राणशब्देन आयुः

### Saṃhitā

परा॒ वा ए॒तस्यायुः॑ प्रा॒ण ए॑ति॒ योऽꣳ॑शुं गृ॒ह्णात्या नः॑ प्रा॒ण ए॑तु परा॒वत॒ इत्या॒हायु॑रे॒व प्रा॒णमा॒त्मन्ध॑त्ते ।

### Mūla Translation

The life-breath of one who takes the Aṃśu departs. By saying, “May our prāṇa come to us from afar,” he places life, prāṇa itself, within himself.

### Sāyaṇa

प्राणशब्देनाऽऽयुर्विवक्षितम् ।

यो यजमानोऽंशुनामकं सोमरसं पात्रे गृह्णात्येतस्याऽऽयुः प्राणः परैति गच्छति । अतो मन्त्रेणाऽऽयुष्प्रदं प्राणमात्मन्येव स्थापयति ।

### Sāyaṇa Translation

Here the word prāṇa is intended to mean āyus, life. When the yajamāna takes the Soma juice called Aṃśu, his life is regarded as departing. The mantra restores and establishes that life-giving prāṇa within him.

## व्याननम् हिरण्यस्योपरि

### Saṃhitā

अ॒मृत॑मसि प्रा॒णाय॒ त्वेति॒ हिर॑ण्यम॒भि व्य॑निति ।

### Mūla Translation

With “You are immortality, for prāṇa I take you,” he breathes over the gold.

### Sāyaṇa

श्वासवायोर्बहिः परित्यागः प्राणनम् । अन्तराकर्षणमपाननम् । माध्यस्थेन धारणं व्यानम् । “अथ यः प्राणापानयोः संधिः स व्यानः” इति श्रुत्यन्तरात् ।

हिरण्यस्योपरि व्याननेन स्वात्मन्यायुर्धारयति ।

### Sāyaṇa Translation

The outward release of breath is prāṇana. Drawing it inward is apānana. Holding it in the intermediate state is vyāna. Sāyaṇa cites another śruti: “That which is the junction of prāṇa and apāna is vyāna.”

By this vyāna over the gold, the sacrificer retains life within himself.

## अप उपस्पृशति

### Saṃhitā

अप॑ उपस्पृशति भेष॒जं वा आपः॑ ।

### Mūla Translation

He touches water. The Waters are indeed healing.

### Sāyaṇa

“आपो वै शान्ताः” इत्यन्यत्र श्रुतत्वादपां भेषजत्वम् ।

### Sāyaṇa Translation

The Waters are called healing because another śruti states, “The Waters are peaceful.”

## मीमांसा: अदाभ्यांशु, यागौ वा ग्रहौ?

### Saṃhitā

अदाभ्यं गृहीत्वा सोमाय यजेत । अंशुं गृह्णाति ।

### Sāyaṇa

अत्र मीमांसा ।

यददाभ्यं गृहीत्वेति गृह्णात्यंशुमिति द्वयम् ।
तद्यागो वा गुणो यागः स्याददाभ्यांशुनामतः ॥
ग्रहयोरेव नाम स्यादानन्तर्याद्विधिस्तयोः ।
गुणोऽतस्तस्य वाक्येन ज्योतिष्टोमाभिगामिता ॥

भवत्वदाभ्यांशुशब्दयोर्नामत्वम् । ते च नामनी ग्रहयोरेव स्यातां न तु यागयोः, गृहीत्वेति शब्दस्यानन्तरमेव पाठात् । यजतिस्तु व्यवहितः, अंशुवाक्ये तु यजिरपि नास्ति ।

तस्माद्ग्रहयोरत्र विधिः । ग्रहणं च ज्योतिष्टोमगतस्य सोमरसस्य संस्काररूपो गुणः । तस्माज्ज्योतिष्टोमे गुणविधिः ।

### Sāyaṇa Translation

The Mīmāṃsā question is whether Adābhya and Aṃśu designate new sacrifices or grahas within the Jyotiṣṭoma.

The siddhānta accepts that they are names, but holds that they are names of grahas, not independent yāgas. The word gṛhītvā occurs immediately with Adābhya, while yajeta is more remote. In the Aṃśu sentence there is no verb of sacrificing at all.

Therefore the prescription concerns the grahas. Their taking is a guṇa in the form of a saṃskāra of Soma juice belonging to the Jyotiṣṭoma.

### Notes

#### Mīmāṃsā: Core conclusion

नाम → ग्रहत्वम् → ज्योतिष्टोमसम्बन्धः → गुणविधिः.

## मीमांसा: सामान्यग्रहधर्माः

### Saṃhitā

—

### Sāyaṇa

“ग्रहाः साद्यन्ते” इत्यादिवाक्येन ग्रहधर्मत्वावगमे तयोरपि ग्रहयोरनिवार्यत्वात् । न चैन्द्रवायवादयः प्रकरणिनः, ज्योतिष्टोमो हि प्रकरणी । तस्मात्सन्ति तयोर्धर्माः ।

### Sāyaṇa Translation

Generic statements such as “the grahas are placed” establish these as dharmas of grahas as a class. Once Aṃśu and Adābhya are established as grahas, the standard graha-dharmas apply to them.

The governing context is not merely the Aindravāyava and related individual grahas. The true prakaraṇin is the Jyotiṣṭoma itself.

## Integrated Detailed Study Notes

### NOTE

This anuvāka is the **Brāhmaṇa explanation of the mantra sequence in 3.3.3**, and almost every explanation reveals a single underlying concern: the ritual actions surrounding the Adābhya and Aṃśu grahas are made to secure **rain, life, and restoration**.

The sequence is especially clear:

**secret names of Waters → rain**\
**night + solar rays → rain**\
**Indra's kakuha-rūpa → rain**\
**Adābhya offering → Soma offered through Soma-like havis**\
**Aṃśu graha → temporary departure of prāṇa**\
**gold → immortality → restoration of life**\
**water-touching → bheṣaja, healing**

The identification of the twelve names beginning with **Māndā** as **गुह्य नामधेय**, secret names, is especially significant. Sāyaṇa understands the efficacy here through the principle that addressing a deity by its hidden or proper sacred name produces satisfaction of the deity, and thereby the desired result : in this case rainfall.

The explanation of **रात्रिः as अह्नो रूपम्** is also unusual. Sāyaṇa does not mean that night is visually the “form” of day. Rather, night **defines the boundary and measure of day**:

**एतावदहः, नातोऽधिकम्**

“The day extends this far, and no farther.”

Thus night becomes the delimiting form by which day is known.

### अत्र मीमांसा

द्वितीयाध्यायस्य तृतीयपादे चिन्तितम् :

यददाभ्यं गृहीत्वेति गृह्णात्यंशुमिति द्वयम् ।\
तद्यागो वा गुणो यागः स्याददाभ्यांशुनामतः ॥

ग्रहयोरेव नाम स्यादानन्तर्याद्विधिस्तयोः ।\
गुणोऽतस्तस्य वाक्येन ज्योतिष्टोमाभिगामिता ॥

A question is considered in the third pāda of the second adhyāya:

When the Śruti says:

**“having taken the Adābhya, he sacrifices to Soma”**

and

**“he takes the Aṃśu,”**

do **Adābhya** and **Aṃśu** designate two new sacrifices, or are they merely names of grahas, subsidiary features within an already established sacrifice?



---



अनारभ्य श्रूयते : “अदाभ्यं गृहीत्वा सोमाय यजेत” इति, “अंशुं गृह्णाति” इति च ।

These statements are taught without an explicitly stated ritual context:

“Having taken the Adābhya, he should sacrifice to Soma,”

and:

“He takes the Aṃśu.”

तत्रादाभ्यशब्दस्य ज्योतिरादिवदपूर्वनामत्वात्तन्नामको यागो यजत इत्याख्यातेन विधीयते ।

One might argue that **Adābhya**, like names such as Jyotis, designates a new and otherwise unknown sacrifice, and that the verb **yajeta**, “he should sacrifice,” prescribes a sacrifice bearing that name.

अंशुमित्यत्र यजतेरश्रवणेऽपि नामविशेषबलादेवापूर्वयागविधिः ।

Similarly, even though the verb “to sacrifice” is not heard in the Aṃśu statement, the special name **Aṃśu** might itself be taken as indicating another new sacrifice.

न चात्र द्रव्यदेवतयोरभावः ।

Nor would the absence of an explicitly stated substance and deity be an obstacle.

ग्रहणालिङ्गेन ज्योतिष्टोमाविकृतित्वावगतो तदीयविध्यन्तातिदेशेन तत्सिद्धेः ।

For from the indication supplied by the act of graha-taking, one could understand these rites as modifications of the Jyotiṣṭoma, and their required substances and deities could then be obtained by atideśa from that sacrifice.

इति प्राप्ते ब्रूमः :

To this prima facie position the conclusion replies:

भवत्वदाभ्यांशुशब्दयोर्नामत्वम् ।

Granted, **Adābhya** and **Aṃśu** are indeed names.

ते च नामनी ग्रहयोरेव स्यातां न तु यागयोः ।

But they are names of **grahas**, not of separate sacrifices.

गृहीत्वेति शब्दस्यानन्तरमेव पाठात् ।

This follows from the fact that the word **gṛhītvā**, “having taken,” occurs immediately adjacent to Adābhya.

यजतिस्तु व्यवहितः ।

The verb **yajati/yajeta**, however, is separated from it.

तादृशोऽपि यजिरंशुवाक्ये नास्ति ।

And in the Aṃśu passage there is not even such a verb of sacrifice.

तस्माद्ग्रहयोरत्र विधिः ।

Therefore what is prescribed here is the taking of two **grahas**.

ग्रहणं च ज्योतिष्टोमगतस्य सोमरसस्य संस्काररूपो गुणः ।

The act of taking the graha is a **guṇa**, a ritual modification or saṃskāra of the Soma juice belonging to the Jyotiṣṭoma.

ऐन्द्रवायवादिग्रहणसमानरूपत्वात् ।

This follows because it is structurally similar to the taking of established grahas such as the Aindravāyava.

यद्यप्यप्रकृतो ज्योतिष्टोमस्तथाऽपि तत्संबन्धिग्रहणद्वारा वाक्याज्ज्योतिष्टोमगामित्वम् ।

Even though the Jyotiṣṭoma is not explicitly under discussion in the immediate context, the reference to graha-taking connects the statement to the Jyotiṣṭoma.

अत एव सोमार्थमदाभ्यं गृहीत्वेति निर्दिश्यते ।

For this very reason the text speaks of “having taken the Adābhya for Soma.”

अथवा तैत्तिरीयाणां षष्ठकाण्डस्य षष्ठप्रपाठके प्राकरणिकं विनियोजकं वाक्यं द्रष्टव्यम् ।

Alternatively, the contextual statement assigning these grahas to the Soma sacrifice may be found in the sixth kāṇḍa, sixth prapāṭhaka of the Taittirīya tradition.

तस्माज्ज्योतिष्टोमे गुणविधिः ।

Therefore this is a **guṇa-vidhi within the Jyotiṣṭoma**, not the prescription of an independent sacrifice.

#### द्वितीया मीमांसा : ग्रहधर्माः

तृतीयाध्यायस्य षष्ठे पादे चिन्तितम् :

ग्रहधर्मा अनारभ्योक्तांश्वदाभ्यद्वये नहि ।\
विद्यन्ते वा प्रकरणादाद्यो वाक्यादिहान्तिमः ॥

A further question is considered in the sixth pāda of the third adhyāya:

Do the usual **graha-dharmas**, such as placing the vessel down, wiping it, and similar procedures, apply to the Aṃśu and Adābhya grahas, even though these two are taught outside the main graha context?

अनारभ्य द्वौ ग्रहौ श्रुतावंशुं गृह्णातीति, अदाभ्यं गृहीत्वेति च ।

The two grahas are taught independently:

“He takes the Aṃśu,”

and:

“Having taken the Adābhya.”

तयोः सादनसंमार्गादयो ग्रहधर्मा न विद्यन्तेऽनारभ्याधीतेषु ।

One might argue that graha-dharmas such as **sādana** and **saṃmārga** do not apply to them because they are taught outside the graha-prakaraṇa.

ऐन्द्रवायवादिष्वेव प्रकरणेन व्यवस्थापितत्वात् ।

Those graha-dharmas, one might say, are contextually restricted to grahas such as the Aindravāyava.

इति चेन्मैवम् ।

But this is not so.

“ग्रहाः साद्यन्ते” इत्यादिवाक्येन ग्रहधर्मत्वावगमे तयोरपि ग्रहयोरनिवार्यत्वात् ।

Statements such as:

**“The grahas are set down”**

establish these actions as **graha-dharmas generally**.

Therefore they necessarily apply to these two grahas as well.

न चैन्द्रवायवादयः प्रकरणिनः ।

Nor are the Aindravāyava and the other individual grahas themselves the primary subject of the section.

ज्योतिष्टोमो हि प्रकरणी ।

The actual **prakaraṇin**, the governing ritual context, is the **Jyotiṣṭoma**.

तस्मात्सन्ति तयोर्धर्माः ॥

Therefore the ordinary graha-dharmas **do apply** to both the Aṃśu and Adābhya grahas.

##### NOTE ON THE MĪMĀṂSĀ

The two discussions establish two different things.

First:

**Adābhya and Aṃśu are not independent sacrifices.**\
They are names of **grahas**, and their taking is a guṇa or saṃskāra within the Jyotiṣṭoma.

Second:

Because they are genuinely **grahas**, they inherit the ordinary **graha-dharmas** even though their individual prescriptions occur outside the main graha section.

So the reasoning is:

**नामविशेषः → ग्रहत्वम् → ज्योतिष्टोमसम्बन्धः → सामान्यग्रहधर्मप्राप्तिः**

The special name identifies the graha; graha-status connects it to the Jyotiṣṭoma; and that status in turn brings with it the general procedural rules belonging to grahas.

**इति श्रीमत्सायणाचार्यविरचिते माधवीये वेदार्थप्रकाशे कृष्णयजुर्वेदीयतैत्तिरीयसंहिताभाष्ये तृतीयकाण्डे तृतीयप्रपाठके चतुर्थोऽनुवाकः ॥४॥**

### अत्र मीमांसा

द्वितीयाध्यायस्य तृतीयपादे चिन्तितम् :

यददाभ्यं गृहीत्वेति गृह्णात्यंशुमिति द्वयम् ।\
तद्यागो वा गुणो यागः स्याददाभ्यांशुनामतः ॥

ग्रहयोरेव नाम स्यादानन्तर्याद्विधिस्तयोः ।\
गुणोऽतस्तस्य वाक्येन ज्योतिष्टोमाभिगामिता ॥

A question is considered in the third pāda of the second adhyāya:

When the Śruti says:

**“having taken the Adābhya, he sacrifices to Soma”**

and

**“he takes the Aṃśu,”**

do **Adābhya** and **Aṃśu** designate two new sacrifices, or are they merely names of grahas, subsidiary features within an already established sacrifice?



---



अनारभ्य श्रूयते : “अदाभ्यं गृहीत्वा सोमाय यजेत” इति, “अंशुं गृह्णाति” इति च ।

These statements are taught without an explicitly stated ritual context:

“Having taken the Adābhya, he should sacrifice to Soma,”

and:

“He takes the Aṃśu.”

तत्रादाभ्यशब्दस्य ज्योतिरादिवदपूर्वनामत्वात्तन्नामको यागो यजत इत्याख्यातेन विधीयते ।

One might argue that **Adābhya**, like names such as Jyotis, designates a new and otherwise unknown sacrifice, and that the verb **yajeta**, “he should sacrifice,” prescribes a sacrifice bearing that name.

अंशुमित्यत्र यजतेरश्रवणेऽपि नामविशेषबलादेवापूर्वयागविधिः ।

Similarly, even though the verb “to sacrifice” is not heard in the Aṃśu statement, the special name **Aṃśu** might itself be taken as indicating another new sacrifice.

न चात्र द्रव्यदेवतयोरभावः ।

Nor would the absence of an explicitly stated substance and deity be an obstacle.

ग्रहणालिङ्गेन ज्योतिष्टोमाविकृतित्वावगतो तदीयविध्यन्तातिदेशेन तत्सिद्धेः ।

For from the indication supplied by the act of graha-taking, one could understand these rites as modifications of the Jyotiṣṭoma, and their required substances and deities could then be obtained by atideśa from that sacrifice.

इति प्राप्ते ब्रूमः :

To this prima facie position the conclusion replies:

भवत्वदाभ्यांशुशब्दयोर्नामत्वम् ।

Granted, **Adābhya** and **Aṃśu** are indeed names.

ते च नामनी ग्रहयोरेव स्यातां न तु यागयोः ।

But they are names of **grahas**, not of separate sacrifices.

गृहीत्वेति शब्दस्यानन्तरमेव पाठात् ।

This follows from the fact that the word **gṛhītvā**, “having taken,” occurs immediately adjacent to Adābhya.

यजतिस्तु व्यवहितः ।

The verb **yajati/yajeta**, however, is separated from it.

तादृशोऽपि यजिरंशुवाक्ये नास्ति ।

And in the Aṃśu passage there is not even such a verb of sacrifice.

तस्माद्ग्रहयोरत्र विधिः ।

Therefore what is prescribed here is the taking of two **grahas**.

ग्रहणं च ज्योतिष्टोमगतस्य सोमरसस्य संस्काररूपो गुणः ।

The act of taking the graha is a **guṇa**, a ritual modification or saṃskāra of the Soma juice belonging to the Jyotiṣṭoma.

ऐन्द्रवायवादिग्रहणसमानरूपत्वात् ।

This follows because it is structurally similar to the taking of established grahas such as the Aindravāyava.

यद्यप्यप्रकृतो ज्योतिष्टोमस्तथाऽपि तत्संबन्धिग्रहणद्वारा वाक्याज्ज्योतिष्टोमगामित्वम् ।

Even though the Jyotiṣṭoma is not explicitly under discussion in the immediate context, the reference to graha-taking connects the statement to the Jyotiṣṭoma.

अत एव सोमार्थमदाभ्यं गृहीत्वेति निर्दिश्यते ।

For this very reason the text speaks of “having taken the Adābhya for Soma.”

अथवा तैत्तिरीयाणां षष्ठकाण्डस्य षष्ठप्रपाठके प्राकरणिकं विनियोजकं वाक्यं द्रष्टव्यम् ।

Alternatively, the contextual statement assigning these grahas to the Soma sacrifice may be found in the sixth kāṇḍa, sixth prapāṭhaka of the Taittirīya tradition.

तस्माज्ज्योतिष्टोमे गुणविधिः ।

Therefore this is a **guṇa-vidhi within the Jyotiṣṭoma**, not the prescription of an independent sacrifice.



---



#### द्वितीया मीमांसा : ग्रहधर्माः

तृतीयाध्यायस्य षष्ठे पादे चिन्तितम् :

ग्रहधर्मा अनारभ्योक्तांश्वदाभ्यद्वये नहि ।\
विद्यन्ते वा प्रकरणादाद्यो वाक्यादिहान्तिमः ॥

A further question is considered in the sixth pāda of the third adhyāya:

Do the usual **graha-dharmas**, such as placing the vessel down, wiping it, and similar procedures, apply to the Aṃśu and Adābhya grahas, even though these two are taught outside the main graha context?

अनारभ्य द्वौ ग्रहौ श्रुतावंशुं गृह्णातीति, अदाभ्यं गृहीत्वेति च ।

The two grahas are taught independently:

“He takes the Aṃśu,”

and:

“Having taken the Adābhya.”

तयोः सादनसंमार्गादयो ग्रहधर्मा न विद्यन्तेऽनारभ्याधीतेषु ।

One might argue that graha-dharmas such as **sādana** and **saṃmārga** do not apply to them because they are taught outside the graha-prakaraṇa.

ऐन्द्रवायवादिष्वेव प्रकरणेन व्यवस्थापितत्वात् ।

Those graha-dharmas, one might say, are contextually restricted to grahas such as the Aindravāyava.

इति चेन्मैवम् ।

But this is not so.

“ग्रहाः साद्यन्ते” इत्यादिवाक्येन ग्रहधर्मत्वावगमे तयोरपि ग्रहयोरनिवार्यत्वात् ।

Statements such as:

**“The grahas are set down”**

establish these actions as **graha-dharmas generally**.

Therefore they necessarily apply to these two grahas as well.

न चैन्द्रवायवादयः प्रकरणिनः ।

Nor are the Aindravāyava and the other individual grahas themselves the primary subject of the section.

ज्योतिष्टोमो हि प्रकरणी ।

The actual **prakaraṇin**, the governing ritual context, is the **Jyotiṣṭoma**.

तस्मात्सन्ति तयोर्धर्माः ॥

Therefore the ordinary graha-dharmas **do apply** to both the Aṃśu and Adābhya grahas.

##### NOTE ON THE MĪMĀṂSĀ

The two discussions establish two different things.

First:

**Adābhya and Aṃśu are not independent sacrifices.**\
They are names of **grahas**, and their taking is a guṇa or saṃskāra within the Jyotiṣṭoma.

Second:

Because they are genuinely **grahas**, they inherit the ordinary **graha-dharmas** even though their individual prescriptions occur outside the main graha section.

So the reasoning is:

**नामविशेषः → ग्रहत्वम् → ज्योतिष्टोमसम्बन्धः → सामान्यग्रहधर्मप्राप्तिः**

The special name identifies the graha; graha-status connects it to the Jyotiṣṭoma; and that status in turn brings with it the general procedural rules belonging to grahas.

**इति श्रीमत्सायणाचार्यविरचिते माधवीये वेदार्थप्रकाशे कृष्णयजुर्वेदीयतैत्तिरीयसंहिताभाष्ये तृतीयकाण्डे तृतीयप्रपाठके चतुर्थोऽनुवाकः ॥४॥**
