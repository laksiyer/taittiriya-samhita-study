# Taittirīya Saṃhitā 3.1.3

## Complete Anuvāka

### Saṃhitā

य॒ज्ञं वा ए॒तथ्सं भ॑रन्ति॒ यथ्सो॑म॒क्रय॑ण्यै प॒दं य॑ज्ञमु॒खꣳ  ह॑वि॒र्धाने॒ यर्हि॑ हवि॒र्धाने॒ प्राची᳚ प्रव॒र्तये॑यु॒स्तर्हि॒  तेनाक्ष॒मुपा᳚ञ्ज्याद्यज्ञमु॒ख ए॒व य॒ज्ञमनु॒ सं त॑नोति॒ प्राञ्च॑म॒ग्निं प्र  ह॑र॒न्त्युत्पत्नी॒मा न॑य॒न्त्यन्वनाꣳ॑सि॒ प्र व॑र्तय॒न्त्यथ॒ वा अ॑स्यै॒ष  धिष्णि॑यो हीयते॒ सोऽनु॑ ध्यायति॒ स ई᳚श्व॒रो रु॒द्रो भू॒त्वा  १० प्र॒जां प॒शून्, यज॑मानस्य॒ शम॑यितो॒र्यर्हि॑ प॒शुमाप्री॑त॒मुद॑ञ्चं॒  नय॑न्ति॒ तर्हि॒ तस्य॑ पशु॒श्रप॑णꣳ हरे॒त् तेनै॒वैनं॑ भा॒गिनं॑  करोति॒ यज॑मानो॒ वा आ॑हव॒नीयो॒ यज॑मानं॒ वा ए॒तद्वि क॑र्षन्ते॒  यदा॑हव॒नीया᳚त् पशु॒श्रप॑ण॒ꣳ॒ हर॑न्ति॒ स वै॒व स्यान्नि॑र्म॒न्थ्यं॑  वा कुर्या॒द्यज॑मानस्य सात्म॒त्वाय॒ यदि॑ प॒शोर॑व॒दानं॒ नश्ये॒दाज्य॑स्य  प्रत्या॒ख्याय॒मव॑ द्ये॒थ्सैव ततः॒ प्राय॑श्चित्ति॒र्ये प॒शुं वि॑मथ्नी॒रन्, यस्तान्  का॒मये॒तार्ति॒मार्च्छे॑यु॒रिति॑ कु॒विद॒ङ्गेति॒ नमो॑ वृक्तिवत्य॒र्चाग्नी᳚ध्रे  जुहुया॒न्नमो॑ वृक्तिमे॒वैषां᳚ वृङ्क्ते ता॒जगार्ति॒मार्च्छ॑न्ति

### Mūla Translation

The footprint of the Soma-purchasing cow is gathered as a form of the sacrifice itself. When the two havirdhāna carts are turned eastward, the axle is anointed with the ghee-anointed earth from that footprint, thus extending the sacrifice at the very mouth of the sacrifice.

Fire is carried eastward, the wife is led forward, and the carts move east. In this movement the old Gārhapatya place is left behind. The fire of that place may become Rudra-like and dangerous to the sacrificer's progeny and cattle, so when the appeased animal is led northward the paśu-cooking fire is taken from that fire, giving it a share.

The paśu-cooking fire should not simply be drawn from the Āhavanīya in a way that symbolically pulls apart the yajamāna. It may instead be taken in the prescribed manner or generated by churning, preserving the yajamāna's wholeness.

If a sacrificial portion from the animal is lost, an equal number of portions is cut from ghee as expiation. A further kāmya prescription concerns opponents who quarrel over or seize an animal: the sacrificer who desires their distress offers the namovṛkti verse at the Āgnīdhra.

### Sāyaṇa Translation / Explanation

Sāyaṇa identifies the ghee-anointed earth from the Soma-purchasing cow's footprint as a gathered form of the sacrifice. Applying it to the cart axle when the havirdhānas move eastward extends the sacrifice at its own mouth.

He reads the abandoned old Gārhapatya place as ritually dangerous. The fire left behind broods, may become Rudra-like, and may injure progeny and cattle. Giving it a share through the animal-cooking fire prevents this.

The warning against drawing that fire directly from the Āhavanīya rests on identification of the Āhavanīya with the yajamāna: pulling fire away would symbolically pull the sacrificer apart. Churning a fresh fire avoids that defect.

For a lost animal portion he prescribes corresponding ghee portions as prāyaścitta. The final hostile kāmya use of the namovṛkti verse is explained as stripping opponents of social honor and salutation.

### Notes

#### Study note

The old Gārhapatya is treated as potentially Rudra-like when its place is abandoned.

#### Study note

Lost paśu portions are replaced by corresponding ghee portions as prāyaścitta.
