# Taittirīya Saṃhitā 3.1.4

## Complete Anuvāka

### Saṃhitā

### Mūla Translation

All creatures, those being born and those already born, belong to Prajāpati. The animal is asked to report itself to Prajāpati, who knows and may approve. The sacrificer then addresses Agni as Paśupati: “Today I bind this animal for you in the midst of a well-performed rite. Approve; may we sacrifice well; may this oblation be dear to the gods.”

The earlier gods knowingly receive the animal's life-breath as it moves beyond its limbs. The animal is told to go to heaven by the god-travelled paths, while its bodily forms are re-established in the plants. The animal, purchased from Paśupati, is to reach its sacrificial share, and wealth and nourishment are requested for the yajamāna.

The animal's relatives and fellow herd-members who watch it being bound are released mentally and visually by Agni and Prajāpati. The wild animals of many forms are likewise released by Vāyu and Prajāpati. The gods are asked, while releasing the seed of the world, to grant the yajamāna the path to heaven. The animal prepared for sacrifice becomes food and life for the gods.

The yajamāna's prāṇa is explicitly separated from that of the animal. If the animal cries or strikes its breast with its feet, Agni is asked to release the sacrificer from the resulting fault. The śamitṛs are summoned; the animal is released from the noose and the yajamāna from the fault of bondage. Aditi releases the noose. In an optional hostile formula the noose is transferred to the hated enemy.

Agni is then praised as carrier and preparer of the oblation. Jātavedas is asked to go with the vapā to the gods, to strengthen their bodies with ghee, and to let the gods consume the svāhā-offered oblation. Offerings before and after the vapā honor the two classes of gods associated with the two positions of svāhā.

### Sāyaṇa Translation / Explanation

Sāyaṇa gives the vinayoga of each paśu mantra in detail. Prajāpati is informed of the animal because all creatures are his offspring; Agni is addressed as Paśupati and asked to approve the binding. The next five offerings release the animal's life-breath, its relatives, fellow herd-members, and wild-animal correspondences while directing the sacrificial essence toward heaven and the gods.

The formula separating the yajamāna's prāṇa from that of the animal is crucial. Without that separation, following the animal toward death would endanger the sacrificer. The animal's cry or violent movement at death generates possible ritual fault, and Agni is asked to release the sacrificer from it.

Sāyaṇa interprets the noose-release formulas literally and ritually, then explains the offerings over and around the vapā as restoring sacrificial medhas to Agni and satisfying the two groups of deities associated with svāhā before and after the main vapā offering.

His Mīmāṃsā discussion on the paśa mantra is technically important: it analyzes singular, dual, and plural wording under atideśa and ūha, and argues about whether a plural form may remain unchanged or must be adjusted to the number actually present.
