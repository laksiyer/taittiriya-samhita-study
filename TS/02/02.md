# Taittirīya Saṃhitā 3.2.2

## Complete Anuvāka

### Mūla Translation

There are three savanas, but the third pressing would be defective if it had no Soma stalk. Therefore, after the Upāṃśu graha is offered, an unpressed Soma stalk is placed in the Upāṃśu vessel and preserved until the third savana. There it is mixed with the exhausted Soma material and pressed. Through swelling it has a Soma stalk, and through pressing it has expressed juice, making all three savanas alike in vitality and brilliance.

The Pūtabhṛt and Ādhavanīya are praised as two extended, undecaying oceans that alternate like waves and like day and night. People cross one while seeing and the other as though by a bridge without seeing. The Dronakalaśa is compared with the Sun, who wears two continuous garments, dark night and bright day, concealing and revealing himself.

The gods and Asuras once performed the same sacrificial actions. To obtain victory the gods concealed the great Soma sacrifice behind publicly visible rites. They outwardly performed Agnihotra, Pūrṇamāsa, Darśa, Vaiśvadeva, Varuṇapraghāsa, Sākamedha, Pitṛyajña, and Tryambaka rites while inwardly carrying out corresponding parts of the Soma sacrifice. The Asuras were deceived and could not follow it. Because the sacrifice could not be harmed, it became adhvara. One who knows and performs Soma in this way prospers, while the rival is overcome.

### Sāyaṇa Translation / Explanation

Sāyaṇa explains why the third savana would be deficient if it contained only ṛjīṣa, exhausted Soma material. A genuine unpressed aṃśu is preserved from the morning and pressed at the third savana, making all three pressings Soma-bearing, bright, and equal in vīrya.

The two ocean verse is applied to Pūtabhṛt and Ādhavanīya, while the two-garment solar verse is applied to Dronakalaśa. He develops the day-night and hidden-visible symbolism in detail.

The long Deva-Asura narrative explains the Soma sacrifice as a concealed mahāyajña. The gods publicly display familiar haviryajñas while secretly performing corresponding Soma components, preventing the Asuras from tracking the rite. Hence adhvara is interpreted as the sacrifice that cannot be harmed.

#### Study note

The third savana is restored to true savana-status by preserving and repressing an actual Soma aṃśu.

#### Study note

The gods conceal the Soma sacrifice behind visible haviryajñas, producing the nirvacana of adhvara as what cannot be harmed.

## Expanded Study Notes from the Working Commentary

### Notes

#### Study note: The ritual problem of the third savana


The morning and midday savanas press fresh Soma. The third savana normally presses the already-worked Soma residue, `ऋजीष`.

Sāyaṇa therefore raises a precise problem:

if no fresh Soma stalk is pressed, how can the third rite fully satisfy the meaning of *savana*, an occasion on which Soma is pressed?

The solution is to preserve an unpressed Soma stalk in the Upāṃśu vessel after the morning rite. At the third savana it is added to the ṛjīṣa, swollen with Vasatīvarī water, and actually pressed.

This produces three parallel qualities:

`अंशुमन्ति`

all three savanas possess a Soma stalk,

`शुक्रवन्ति`

all possess Soma juice,

`समावद्वीर्याणि`

all become equal in ritual potency.

#### Study note: Pūtabhṛt and Ādhavanīya as the two oceans


The mantra `द्वौ समुद्रौ` is applied to Pūtabhṛt and Ādhavanīya.

Sāyaṇa superimposes two images on them:

two inexhaustible oceans,

and day and night.

Their use alternates as waves alternate in the belly of the sea.

The Pūtabhṛt is visible and therefore corresponds to the day, which people cross while seeing.

The Ādhavanīya is placed in a concealed location and therefore corresponds to the night, which people cross without seeing, as though over a bridge.

#### Study note: The Droṇakalaśa as the Sun with two garments


The second mantra is applied to the Droṇakalaśa.

The one `केशी`, the long-haired one, is Sūrya, whose rays are like hair.

His two uninterrupted garments are day and night.

The dark garment conceals him.

When old night is left behind, he assumes the bright garment of day.

The Droṇakalaśa is compared with this solar alternation because it is ordinarily hidden beneath the Havirdhāna and later brought forth and made visible.

#### Study note: The concealed Soma sacrifice


The second half of the anuvāka has a different movement.

The gods recognize the great Soma sacrifice, while the Asuras imitate whatever they can observe. The gods therefore outwardly display familiar Haviryajñas while secretly performing the corresponding Soma-sacrifice elements.

Sāyaṇa's correspondences are:

**Agnihotra outwardly → dīkṣā-vrata secretly**

**Paurṇamāsa → Agnīṣomīya animal**

**Darśa → Āgneya Savanīya animal**

**Vaiśvadeva → morning savana**

**Varuṇapraghāsas → midday savana**

**Sākamedhas + Pitṛyajña + Tryambakas → third savana**

The visible rites function as concealment. The Asuras follow the outer appearance and fail to discover the actual Soma sacrifice.

#### Study note: Adhvara


It is in this concealment narrative that Sāyaṇa explains the word **adhvara**.

The gods become:

`अध्वर्तव्याः`

not capable of being injured by the Asuras.

The sacrifice through which they cannot be `ध्वरित`, harmed, is therefore called **adhvara**.

The point is not merely a general etymology. The name arises within the specific narrative logic of making the sacrifice undiscoverable and therefore invulnerable.
